Section A — Multiple choice (1 mark each). Questions 13–16 are Assertion–Reason.

Q11 mark
Two electric field lines never intersect each other because
  1. the field is zero at the point of intersection
  2. the field would have two directions at the same point
  3. like charges repel
  4. field lines are imaginary
Marking scheme
(b) At an intersection the field would have two directions, which is impossible.
Q21 mark
The equipotential surfaces of a point charge are
  1. planes
  2. concentric spheres
  3. coaxial cylinders
  4. ellipsoids
Marking scheme
(b) Concentric spheres centred on the charge.
Q31 mark
A wire of resistance 4 Ω is bent into a circle. The resistance between two diametrically opposite points is
  1. 4 Ω
  2. 2 Ω
  3. 1 Ω
  4. 0.5 Ω
Marking scheme
(c) Two 2 Ω halves in parallel give 1 Ω.
Q41 mark
When the temperature of a metallic conductor increases, the relaxation time of free electrons
  1. increases
  2. decreases
  3. remains unchanged
  4. becomes zero
Marking scheme
(b) More frequent collisions with vibrating ions shorten τ, so resistivity rises.
Q51 mark
A charged particle enters a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to it. Its path is
  1. a straight line
  2. a parabola
  3. a circle
  4. a helix
Marking scheme
(c) qvB provides the centripetal force: r = mv/qB.
Q61 mark
A material with a small negative magnetic susceptibility is
  1. paramagnetic
  2. diamagnetic
  3. ferromagnetic
  4. ferrimagnetic
Marking scheme
(b) Diamagnetic (e.g. bismuth, copper).
Q71 mark
Lenz's law is a consequence of the conservation of
  1. charge
  2. momentum
  3. energy
  4. mass
Marking scheme
(c) Energy: the induced current opposes the change that produces it.
Q81 mark
In a purely inductive AC circuit, the current
  1. is in phase with the voltage
  2. leads the voltage by π/2
  3. lags the voltage by π/2
  4. lags the voltage by π
Marking scheme
(c) It lags the voltage by π/2.
Q91 mark
For an electromagnetic wave in vacuum, the ratio E₀/B₀ equals
  1. c
  2. 1/c
  3. c²
  4. √c
Marking scheme
(a) E₀/B₀ = c.
Q101 mark
The power of a lens of focal length −25 cm is
  1. +4 D
  2. −4 D
  3. −0.25 D
  4. +0.25 D
Marking scheme
(b) P = 100/f(cm) = −4 D (diverging lens).
Q111 mark
In Young's double-slit experiment, if one slit is closed, the screen shows
  1. uniform illumination
  2. the diffraction pattern of a single slit
  3. fringes of double width
  4. no light at all
Marking scheme
(b) Interference disappears; only single-slit diffraction remains.
Q121 mark
The maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons depends on the
  1. intensity of the incident light
  2. frequency of the incident light
  3. time of exposure
  4. area of the metal surface
Marking scheme
(b) K_max = hν − φ₀.
Q131 mark
Assertion (A): The electric potential everywhere inside a charged hollow conducting sphere is constant.
Reason (R): The electric field inside the sphere is zero.
  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
  3. A is true but R is false.
  4. A is false but R is true.
Marking scheme
(a) E = −dV/dr = 0 inside, so V is constant and equal to its surface value.
Q141 mark
Assertion (A): Bohr's model cannot explain the spectra of multi-electron atoms.
Reason (R): Bohr's model ignores electron–electron interactions.
  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
  3. A is true but R is false.
  4. A is false but R is true.
Marking scheme
(a) Electron–electron repulsion is significant in multi-electron atoms.
Q151 mark
Assertion (A): The resistivity of a semiconductor decreases with increase in temperature.
Reason (R): The number of charge carriers increases with temperature.
  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
  3. A is true but R is false.
  4. A is false but R is true.
Marking scheme
(a) More electron–hole pairs form at higher T, which outweighs the shorter relaxation time.
Q161 mark
Assertion (A): A convex lens always forms a real image.
Reason (R): A convex lens converges a parallel beam of light.
  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
  3. A is true but R is false.
  4. A is false but R is true.
Marking scheme
(d) An object inside the focus gives a virtual image (magnifier), so A is false.

Section B — Very short answer (2 marks each)

Q172 marks
State Gauss's law in electrostatics. A charge q is placed at the centre of a cube. Find the electric flux through (i) the whole cube and (ii) one face.
Marking scheme
Total flux through a closed surface = (charge enclosed)/ε₀. (i) Φ = q/ε₀ (ii) by symmetry, each face gets q/6ε₀. [1 + ½ + ½]
Q182 marks
Resistors of 4 Ω and 12 Ω are connected in parallel across a 6 V battery of negligible internal resistance. Find (i) the current drawn from the battery and (ii) the power dissipated in the 4 Ω resistor.
Marking scheme
R_p = (4×12)/16 = 3 Ω; I = 6/3 = 2 A. Current in 4 Ω = 6/4 = 1.5 A; P = V²/R = 36/4 = 9 W. [1 + 1]
Q192 marks
Define mutual inductance. Write its SI unit and two factors on which the mutual inductance of a pair of coils depends.
Marking scheme
M = Φ₂/I₁: flux linked with one coil per unit current in the other. Unit: henry (H). Depends on the number of turns, area and separation/orientation of the coils, and the permeability of the core. [1 + ½ + ½]
Q202 marks
A ray of light passes through an equilateral glass prism of refractive index √2 at minimum deviation. Find the angle of minimum deviation.
Marking scheme
n = sin[(A + δm)/2] / sin(A/2) ⇒ √2 × sin30° = sin[(60° + δm)/2] ⇒ sin[(60° + δm)/2] = 1/√2 ⇒ (60° + δm)/2 = 45° ⇒ δm = 30°. [2]
Q212 marks
An electron and a proton have the same de Broglie wavelength. Which of them has more kinetic energy? Justify.
Marking scheme
λ = h/p, so equal λ means equal momentum. K = p²/2m, so the lighter electron has more kinetic energy (K_e/K_p = m_p/m_e ≈ 1836). [1 + 1]

Section C — Short answer (3 marks each)

Q223 marks
Derive an expression for the electric potential at a point on the axial line of an electric dipole. How does it vary with distance r for r ≫ a?
Marking scheme
Charges −q at −a and +q at +a; point P at distance r on the axis. V = (1/4πε₀)[q/(r − a) − q/(r + a)] = (1/4πε₀)·2qa/(r² − a²) = p/[4πε₀(r² − a²)]. For r ≫ a, V = p/(4πε₀r²), so V ∝ 1/r² (compared with 1/r for a point charge). [2 + 1]
Q233 marks
State Kirchhoff's two rules. Use them to obtain the balance condition of a Wheatstone bridge.
Marking scheme
Junction rule: ΣI = 0 at a junction (charge conservation). Loop rule: Σ(IR) = ΣE around a closed loop (energy conservation). Balanced bridge (I_g = 0): loop ABDA gives I₁P = I₂R and loop BCDB gives I₁Q = I₂S. Dividing, P/Q = R/S. [1 + 2]
Q243 marks
Using the Biot–Savart law, derive the magnetic field on the axis of a circular current loop of radius R at distance x from its centre. Hence write the field at the centre.
Marking scheme
dB = (μ₀/4π) I dl/(x² + R²). Perpendicular components cancel; axial components add: dB cosθ with cosθ = R/√(x² + R²). B = μ₀IR²/[2(x² + R²)^{3/2}] along the axis. At the centre (x = 0), B = μ₀I/2R. [2½ + ½]
Q253 marks
A series LCR circuit with L = 2.0 H, C = 32 μF and R = 10 Ω is connected to a 230 V variable-frequency AC source. Find (i) the resonant angular frequency, (ii) the rms current at resonance and (iii) the Q-factor.
Marking scheme
(i) ω₀ = 1/√(LC) = 1/√(2 × 32 × 10⁻⁶) = 125 rad s⁻¹. (ii) At resonance Z = R, so I_rms = 230/10 = 23 A. (iii) Q = ω₀L/R = (125 × 2)/10 = 25. [1 + 1 + 1]
Q263 marks
Draw a labelled ray diagram of a compound microscope with the final image at the near point. Write the expression for its magnifying power.
Marking scheme
Diagram: objective forms a real, inverted, magnified image A′B′ inside the focus of the eyepiece; the eyepiece forms the final virtual image at D. m = m_o × m_e = (v_o/u_o)(1 + D/f_e) in magnitude; for a short tube length, m ≈ (L/f_o)(D/f_e). [2 + 1]
Q273 marks
Write Einstein's photoelectric equation. Draw the graph of stopping potential against frequency and state what its slope and intercept represent.
Marking scheme
hν = φ₀ + K_max = hν₀ + eV₀. V₀ = (h/e)ν − φ₀/e: a straight line. Slope = h/e (the same for all metals); intercept on the ν-axis = threshold frequency ν₀; intercept on the V-axis = −φ₀/e. [1 + 1 + 1]
Q283 marks
Draw the binding energy per nucleon curve. Use it to explain the release of energy in nuclear fission and fusion.
Marking scheme
The curve rises steeply for light nuclei, peaks at about 8.75 MeV (A ≈ 56, Fe), then falls slowly to about 7.6 MeV for A ≈ 238. Fission: a heavy nucleus (A ≈ 236) splits into middle-mass nuclei with higher BE/A, releasing about 200 MeV. Fusion: light nuclei combine into a nucleus with higher BE/A, releasing energy. [1 + 1 + 1]

Section D — Case-based questions (4 marks each)

Q294 marks
Case study: p-n junction diode. When a p-n junction forms, diffusion of majority carriers creates a depletion region and a barrier potential. Forward bias lowers the barrier and a large current flows; reverse bias raises it and only a tiny current flows. Diodes are used as rectifiers.
(i) What happens to the width of the depletion region in forward bias?
(ii) What is the typical barrier potential of a silicon diode?
(iii) Which charge carriers cause the reverse current?
(iv) A full-wave rectifier is fed with 50 Hz AC. What is the frequency of the ripple in the output?
Marking scheme
(i) It decreases. (ii) About 0.7 V. (iii) Minority carriers. (iv) 100 Hz. [1 × 4]
Q304 marks
Case study: total internal reflection. When light travels from a denser to a rarer medium at an angle of incidence greater than the critical angle, it is totally reflected. This is used in optical fibres, prisms and the sparkle of diamonds.
(i) State the two conditions for total internal reflection.
(ii) Find the critical angle for a medium of refractive index √2.
(iii) On what principle does an optical fibre work?
(iv) Why does a diamond sparkle?
Marking scheme
(i) Light must go from a denser to a rarer medium, and i > i_c. (ii) sin i_c = 1/√2 ⇒ i_c = 45°. (iii) Repeated total internal reflection at the core–cladding boundary. (iv) Diamond has a very small critical angle (about 24.4°), so light entering it is totally reflected many times before leaving. [1 × 4]

Section E — Long answer (5 marks each)

Q315 marks
(a) Derive the capacitance of a parallel-plate capacitor with plate area A and separation d when a dielectric slab of dielectric constant K and thickness t (t < d) is placed between the plates.
(b) A charged capacitor is disconnected from the battery and a dielectric slab (K) fills the space between its plates. How do the capacitance, potential difference and stored energy change?
Marking scheme
(a) E₀ = σ/ε₀ in air, E = E₀/K in the slab. V = E₀(d − t) + (E₀/K)t = (Q/Aε₀)(d − t + t/K). C = Q/V = ε₀A/(d − t + t/K). [3] (b) Q is constant: C becomes KC, V becomes V/K, and U = Q²/2C becomes U/K (energy decreases). [2]
Q325 marks
(a) Explain the principle and working of a moving coil galvanometer. Why is a radial magnetic field used?
(b) A galvanometer of resistance 50 Ω gives full-scale deflection for 2 mA. How will you convert it into a voltmeter of range 0–10 V?
Marking scheme
(a) A current-carrying coil in a magnetic field feels a torque τ = NIAB. A spring gives a restoring torque kφ, so at equilibrium φ = (NAB/k)I and the deflection is proportional to current. A radial field (from concave poles and a soft-iron core) keeps the plane of the coil parallel to B, so the scale is linear. [3] (b) R = V/I_g − G = 10/0.002 − 50 = 4950 Ω, connected in series. [2]
Q335 marks
(a) Derive the expression for fringe width in Young's double-slit experiment.
(b) In a YDSE, λ = 600 nm, slit separation 0.5 mm and screen distance 1 m. Find the fringe width. What happens to it if the apparatus is immersed in water (n = 4/3)?
Marking scheme
(a) Path difference at height y on the screen: Δ = yd/D. Bright fringe: yd/D = nλ ⇒ y_n = nλD/d. Fringe width β = y_{n+1} − y_n = λD/d. [3] (b) β = (600 × 10⁻⁹ × 1)/(0.5 × 10⁻³) = 1.2 mm. In water λ′ = λ/n, so β′ = β/n = 0.9 mm. [2]
