Section B — Very short answer (2 marks each)

Q172 marks
Differentiate between geitonogamy and xenogamy.
Marking scheme
Geitonogamy: pollen goes to the stigma of another flower on the same plant (genetically like selfing; needs an agent). Xenogamy: pollen goes to the stigma of a flower on a different plant, bringing genetically different pollen (true cross-pollination). [2]
Q182 marks
How does a copper-releasing IUD prevent pregnancy? Give one advantage of IUDs.
Marking scheme
Cu ions suppress sperm motility and fertilising capacity and increase phagocytosis of sperm in the uterus. Advantage: long-acting, reversible and ideal for spacing children. [1½ + ½]
Q192 marks
Give two reasons why DNA is a better genetic material than RNA.
Marking scheme
(i) DNA is chemically more stable: no 2′-OH, and it is double-stranded. (ii) It has thymine instead of uracil, so mutations are fewer and repair is possible; RNA mutates faster. [1 + 1]
Q202 marks
Name the causative agent of typhoid and the test used to confirm it. Write one symptom.
Marking scheme
Salmonella typhi; the Widal test. Symptoms: sustained high fever (39–40 °C), weakness, stomach pain, loss of appetite. [½ + ½ + 1]
Q212 marks
What are biofertilisers? Give two examples.
Marking scheme
Organisms that enrich the nutrient quality of the soil. Examples: Rhizobium (symbiotic N₂ fixation), Azotobacter, Anabaena or Nostoc (cyanobacteria), mycorrhiza (Glomus). [1 + 1]

Section C — Short answer (3 marks each)

Q223 marks
Describe the structure of a mature embryo sac. What is double fertilisation?
Marking scheme
7-celled, 8-nucleate: the egg apparatus (egg cell + 2 synergids with filiform apparatus) at the micropylar end, 3 antipodals at the chalazal end, and a central cell with 2 polar nuclei. Double fertilisation: one male gamete + egg → zygote (2n) (syngamy); the other male gamete + 2 polar nuclei → PEN (3n) (triple fusion). [2 + 1]
Q233 marks
Describe the phases of the menstrual cycle and the role of hormones in each.
Marking scheme
Menstrual phase (days 1–5): the endometrium breaks down when progesterone falls. Follicular phase (6–13): FSH and LH drive follicle growth; oestrogen rebuilds the endometrium. Ovulation (about day 14): LH surge. Luteal phase (15–28): the corpus luteum secretes progesterone to maintain the endometrium; without fertilisation it degenerates. [3]
Q243 marks
State the law of independent assortment. Explain it with a dihybrid cross between round yellow (RRYY) and wrinkled green (rryy) pea plants.
Marking scheme
When two pairs of traits are combined, the segregation of one pair is independent of the other. F₁: RrYy (round yellow). F₁ gametes: RY, Ry, rY, ry in equal proportion. F₂: 9 round yellow : 3 round green : 3 wrinkled yellow : 1 wrinkled green. [1 + 2]
Q253 marks
Describe the three steps of transcription in bacteria.
Marking scheme
Initiation: RNA polymerase with the σ factor binds the promoter. Elongation: the polymerase adds ribonucleotides 5′→3′ using the template strand. Termination: the ρ factor helps release the RNA at the terminator. [1 × 3]
Q263 marks
State the Hardy–Weinberg principle. List the five factors that affect genetic equilibrium.
Marking scheme
Allele and genotype frequencies in a population stay constant across generations: p² + 2pq + q² = 1. Factors: gene migration (gene flow), genetic drift, mutation, genetic recombination, natural selection. [1 + 2]
Q273 marks
Describe the three steps of PCR.
Marking scheme
Denaturation: heating to about 94 °C separates the DNA strands. Annealing: primers bind the complementary sequences at about 50–60 °C. Extension: Taq polymerase extends the primers at about 72 °C. Repeated cycles amplify the DNA exponentially. [1 × 3]
Q283 marks
Explain with examples (a) mutualism, (b) competition and (c) brood parasitism.
Marking scheme
(a) Both partners benefit: lichens (fungus + alga), mycorrhiza, fig–wasp. (b) Both are harmed: Gause's competitive exclusion; Abingdon tortoise vs goats in the Galapagos. (c) The cuckoo lays eggs in the crow's nest and the host raises its young. [1 × 3]

Section D — Case-based questions (4 marks each)

Q294 marks
Case study: HIV and AIDS. HIV is a retrovirus spread by sexual contact, infected blood, shared needles and from mother to foetus. It infects helper T-cells and slowly weakens immunity.
(i) Which cells does HIV mainly attack?
(ii) Name the test used to diagnose AIDS.
(iii) Why is HIV called a retrovirus?
(iv) Why do AIDS patients suffer from opportunistic infections?
Marking scheme
(i) Helper T-lymphocytes (CD4⁺), and macrophages. (ii) ELISA. (iii) It has an RNA genome copied into DNA by reverse transcriptase. (iv) Falling helper T-cell numbers cripple immunity, so normally harmless microbes such as Mycobacterium and Toxoplasma cause disease. [1 × 4]
Q304 marks
Case study: ecological pyramids. The number, biomass or energy at successive trophic levels can be drawn as pyramids. By Lindeman's 10% law, only about 10% of energy passes to the next level.
(i) Give an example of an inverted pyramid of biomass.
(ii) Why is the pyramid of energy always upright?
(iii) If producers fix 10,000 J, how much energy reaches the tertiary consumers?
(iv) What is standing crop?
Marking scheme
(i) In the sea, phytoplankton biomass is less than zooplankton/fish biomass. (ii) Energy is lost as heat at every level (second law of thermodynamics). (iii) 10,000 → 1,000 → 100 → 10 J. (iv) The mass of living material at a trophic level at a given time. [1 × 4]

Section E — Long answer (5 marks each)

Q315 marks
Describe spermatogenesis and oogenesis with labelled flow charts. Give two differences between them and explain their hormonal control.
Marking scheme
Spermatogenesis: spermatogonia (2n) → mitosis → primary spermatocytes (2n) → meiosis I → secondary spermatocytes (n) → meiosis II → spermatids → spermiogenesis → sperm (4 per primary spermatocyte). Control: GnRH → LH (Leydig cells, androgens) and FSH (Sertoli cells). Oogenesis: oogonia (fetal life) → primary oocytes arrested in prophase I → at puberty, meiosis I → secondary oocyte + 1st polar body; meiosis II completes only on sperm entry → ovum + 2nd polar body. Differences: 4 gametes vs 1 functional ovum; continuous from puberty vs starts before birth and is cyclic. [5]
Q325 marks
Explain the lac operon. How is it switched off in the absence of lactose and switched on in its presence? Why is it called negative regulation?
Marking scheme
Components: regulator gene i (makes the repressor), promoter, operator, and structural genes z (β-galactosidase), y (permease), a (transacetylase). No lactose: the repressor binds the operator, blocking RNA polymerase, so there is no transcription. Lactose present: allolactose (inducer) binds the repressor and inactivates it, so RNA polymerase transcribes z, y and a. Negative regulation: the repressor switches the operon off. [5]
Q335 marks
What is meant by the 'Evil Quartet'? Explain each cause of biodiversity loss. Differentiate between in-situ and ex-situ conservation with two examples each.
Marking scheme
Evil Quartet: (1) habitat loss and fragmentation (e.g. clearing of rainforests); (2) over-exploitation (Steller's sea cow, passenger pigeon); (3) alien species invasion (Nile perch in Lake Victoria, Parthenium, Lantana, water hyacinth); (4) co-extinctions (host–parasite, plant–pollinator). In-situ protects species in their natural habitat: national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, biosphere reserves, sacred groves, hotspots. Ex-situ protects them outside it: zoos, botanical gardens, seed banks, cryopreservation, tissue culture. [5]
