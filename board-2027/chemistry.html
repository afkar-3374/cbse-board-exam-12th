Section A — Multiple choice (1 mark each). Questions 13–16 are Assertion–Reason.

Q11 mark
Which concentration term does not depend on temperature?
  1. Molarity
  2. Normality
  3. Molality
  4. Formality
Marking scheme
(c) Molality is based on mass, which does not change with temperature.
Q21 mark
The colligative property best suited to find the molar mass of proteins is
  1. relative lowering of vapour pressure
  2. elevation in boiling point
  3. depression in freezing point
  4. osmotic pressure
Marking scheme
(d) Osmotic pressure: it is measured at room temperature and is large even for dilute solutions.
Q31 mark
The unit of the rate constant of a first-order reaction is
  1. mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹
  2. s⁻¹
  3. L mol⁻¹ s⁻¹
  4. L² mol⁻² s⁻¹
Marking scheme
(b) s⁻¹.
Q41 mark
On dilution, the molar conductivity of an electrolyte solution
  1. increases
  2. decreases
  3. stays the same
  4. first decreases then increases
Marking scheme
(a) It increases: more ions per mole (weak electrolytes) and less interionic attraction.
Q51 mark
Which ion has the maximum number of unpaired electrons?
  1. Ti³⁺
  2. V³⁺
  3. Cr³⁺
  4. Mn²⁺
Marking scheme
(d) Mn²⁺ is 3d⁵, with 5 unpaired electrons.
Q61 mark
The lanthanoid that shows a stable +4 oxidation state is

Section B — Very short answer (2 marks each)

Q172 marks
State Raoult's law for a solution of two volatile liquids. What is a positive deviation from it? Give one example.
Marking scheme
p_A = x_A p°_A and p_B = x_B p°_B; p_total = p_A + p_B. Positive deviation: vapour pressure is higher than predicted because A–B interactions are weaker than A–A and B–B; ΔH_mix > 0 and ΔV_mix > 0. Example: ethanol + acetone. [1 + 1]
Q182 marks
For 2A + B → products, rate = k[A][B]². (i) What is the overall order? (ii) How does the rate change if [B] is doubled?
Marking scheme
(i) 1 + 2 = 3. (ii) Rate ∝ [B]², so it becomes 4 times. [1 + 1]
Q192 marks
Calculate ΔG° for the Daniell cell (E°cell = 1.10 V, F = 96500 C mol⁻¹).
Marking scheme
Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu, n = 2. ΔG° = −nFE° = −2 × 96500 × 1.10 = −212300 J mol⁻¹ = −212.3 kJ mol⁻¹. [2]
Q202 marks
Explain: (a) Zn, Cd and Hg are not regarded as transition elements. (b) Cu⁺ is unstable in aqueous solution.
Marking scheme
(a) They have completely filled d¹⁰ orbitals in the ground state and in their common oxidation states. (b) Cu²⁺(aq) has a much more negative hydration enthalpy, so 2Cu⁺ → Cu²⁺ + Cu (disproportionation) is favoured. [1 + 1]
Q212 marks
Write the chemical equation for (a) Rosenmund reduction and (b) Clemmensen reduction.
Marking scheme
(a) RCOCl + H₂ → RCHO + HCl, over Pd/BaSO₄. (b) R₂C=O + 4[H] → R–CH₂–R + H₂O, using Zn–Hg/conc. HCl. [1 + 1]

Section C — Short answer (3 marks each)

Q223 marks
1.8 g of glucose is dissolved in 100 g of water. Calculate the freezing point of the solution (K_f = 1.86 K kg mol⁻¹). What is abnormal molar mass?
Marking scheme
n = 1.8/180 = 0.01 mol; m = 0.01/0.1 = 0.1 mol kg⁻¹. ΔT_f = K_f m = 0.186 K, so T_f = 273.15 − 0.186 = 272.96 K. Abnormal molar mass: a molar mass from colligative data that differs from the true value because the solute associates or dissociates (corrected with the van't Hoff factor i). [2 + 1]
Q233 marks
Show that for a first-order reaction, the time for 99% completion is twice the time for 90% completion.
Marking scheme
t = (2.303/k) log([A]₀/[A]). t₉₉ = (2.303/k) log 100 = (2.303/k) × 2. t₉₀ = (2.303/k) log 10 = (2.303/k) × 1. So t₉₉ = 2 t₉₀. [3]
Q243 marks
Using valence bond theory, explain why [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ is square planar and diamagnetic while [NiCl₄]²⁻ is tetrahedral and paramagnetic.
Marking scheme
Ni²⁺ is 3d⁸. CN⁻ is a strong-field ligand: it pairs the d electrons, leaving one 3d orbital empty, so the hybridisation is dsp² (square planar) with no unpaired electrons (diamagnetic). Cl⁻ is weak-field: no pairing occurs, so the hybridisation is sp³ (tetrahedral) with 2 unpaired electrons (paramagnetic). [1½ + 1½]
Q253 marks
Explain the SN1 mechanism for the hydrolysis of tert-butyl bromide. Why do optically active halides give racemic mixtures by SN1?
Marking scheme
Step 1 (slow): (CH₃)₃C–Br → (CH₃)₃C⁺ + Br⁻. Step 2 (fast): (CH₃)₃C⁺ + OH⁻ → (CH₃)₃COH. Rate = k[RBr] (first order). The carbocation is planar (sp²), so the nucleophile attacks equally from both faces, giving retention and inversion in equal amounts: a racemic mixture. [2 + 1]
Q263 marks
How will you convert (a) phenol to salicylic acid, (b) propanone to propan-2-ol and (c) benzene to acetophenone?
Marking scheme
(a) Kolbe reaction: phenol + NaOH → sodium phenoxide; + CO₂ (4–7 atm, 400 K), then H⁺ → salicylic acid. (b) CH₃COCH₃ + H₂ (Ni/Pt) or NaBH₄ → CH₃CH(OH)CH₃. (c) Friedel–Crafts acylation: C₆H₆ + CH₃COCl (anhydrous AlCl₃) → C₆H₅COCH₃ + HCl. [1 × 3]
Q273 marks
(a) Arrange in increasing order of basic strength in aqueous solution: C₂H₅NH₂, (C₂H₅)₂NH, (C₂H₅)₃N, NH₃. (b) Write the Hoffmann bromamide degradation reaction. (c) Why are aromatic diazonium salts more stable than aliphatic ones?
Marking scheme
(a) NH₃ < C₂H₅NH₂ < (C₂H₅)₃N < (C₂H₅)₂NH. (b) RCONH₂ + Br₂ + 4NaOH → RNH₂ + Na₂CO₃ + 2NaBr + 2H₂O. (c) The diazonium group is resonance-stabilised by the benzene ring. [1 × 3]
Q283 marks
(a) What is the primary structure of a protein? (b) What happens during denaturation? (c) Give two structural differences between DNA and RNA.
Marking scheme
(a) The sequence of amino acids linked by peptide bonds. (b) Secondary and tertiary structure are lost (H-bonds break) on heating or pH change, and biological activity is lost; the primary structure stays intact (e.g. coagulation of egg white). (c) DNA has deoxyribose and thymine and is double-stranded; RNA has ribose and uracil and is single-stranded. [1 × 3]

Section D — Case-based questions (4 marks each)

Q294 marks
Case study: Kohlrausch's law. At infinite dilution every ion migrates independently, and the limiting molar conductivity of an electrolyte equals the sum of the contributions of its ions. This lets us find Λ°m for weak electrolytes.
(i) Define limiting molar conductivity.
(ii) Calculate Λ°m(CH₃COOH), given Λ°m(HCl) = 426.2, Λ°m(NaCl) = 126.4 and Λ°m(CH₃COONa) = 91.0 S cm² mol⁻¹.
(iii) Why does Λm of a weak electrolyte rise steeply on dilution?
(iv) Write the SI unit of conductivity.
Marking scheme
(i) Molar conductivity as concentration approaches zero. (ii) Λ° = 91.0 + 426.2 − 126.4 = 390.8 S cm² mol⁻¹. (iii) The degree of dissociation increases sharply on dilution. (iv) S m⁻¹. [1 × 4]
Q304 marks
Case study: structure of glucose. Several reactions of glucose establish its open-chain structure, while others it fails to give suggest a cyclic structure.
(i) What does the formation of n-hexane with HI show?
(ii) Name the product with bromine water and what it shows.
(iii) What does the formation of a pentaacetate show?
(iv) Which observation supports a cyclic structure?
Marking scheme
(i) The six carbons are in a straight chain. (ii) Gluconic acid: glucose has a –CHO group. (iii) Five –OH groups. (iv) It does not react with NaHSO₃ or give Schiff's test, and its pentaacetate does not react with NH₂OH. [1 × 4]

Section E — Long answer (5 marks each)

Q315 marks
(a) Write the Nernst equation and calculate the emf of the cell Mg | Mg²⁺(0.001 M) || Cu²⁺(0.0001 M) | Cu, given E°cell = 2.71 V.
(b) What is a fuel cell? Write the electrode reactions of the H₂–O₂ fuel cell.
Marking scheme
(a) E = E° − (0.059/n) log([Mg²⁺]/[Cu²⁺]) = 2.71 − (0.059/2) log(10⁻³/10⁻⁴) = 2.71 − 0.0295 = 2.68 V. [3] (b) A galvanic cell that converts the combustion energy of a fuel directly into electricity. Anode: 2H₂ + 4OH⁻ → 4H₂O + 4e⁻. Cathode: O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻. [2]
Q325 marks
(a) How is KMnO₄ prepared from pyrolusite? (b) Write the ionic equation for the oxidation of Fe²⁺ by acidified K₂Cr₂O₇. (c) What is lanthanoid contraction? Give two consequences.
Marking scheme
(a) 2MnO₂ + 4KOH + O₂ → 2K₂MnO₄ + 2H₂O; then 3MnO₄²⁻ + 4H⁺ → 2MnO₄⁻ + MnO₂ + 2H₂O (or electrolytic oxidation). [2] (b) Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6Fe²⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ + 6Fe³⁺ + 7H₂O. [1] (c) The steady decrease in size from La to Lu due to poor shielding by 4f electrons. Consequences: Zr and Hf have similar radii, and basicity decreases from La(OH)₃ to Lu(OH)₃. [2]
Q335 marks
(a) Give chemical tests to distinguish (i) propanal from propanone and (ii) acetophenone from benzophenone.
(b) Write the aldol condensation of ethanal.
(c) Arrange in increasing order of acidity: CH₃COOH, FCH₂COOH, ClCH₂COOH.
Marking scheme
(a)(i) Tollens' reagent: propanal gives a silver mirror, propanone does not. (ii) Iodoform test: acetophenone (CH₃CO–) gives a yellow CHI₃ precipitate, benzophenone does not. [2] (b) 2CH₃CHO (dil. NaOH) → CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CHO (aldol); on heating → CH₃CH=CHCHO (but-2-enal). [2] (c) CH₃COOH < ClCH₂COOH < FCH₂COOH. [1]
