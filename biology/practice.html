2-mark questions

Very short answer.

1.Distinguish autogamy, geitonogamy and xenogamy.

Show answer

Autogamy: pollen to stigma of the same flower. Geitonogamy: to another flower of the same plant (genetically like autogamy). Xenogamy: to a flower of a different plant — brings genetic variation.

2.Why is apomixis useful to farmers?

Show answer

Hybrid characters segregate in the next generation, so hybrid seed must be bought every year. Apomictic hybrids keep hybrid traits without segregation, so farmers can reuse seed.

3.How many cells and nuclei does a typical mature embryo sac have? Name them.

Show answer

7 cells, 8 nuclei: egg apparatus (2 synergids + egg), 3 antipodals, and a central cell with 2 polar nuclei.

4.State the roles of LH and FSH in males.

Show answer

LH acts on Leydig cells to secrete androgens; FSH acts on Sertoli cells and helps spermiogenesis.

5.Give two differences between spermatogenesis and oogenesis.

Show answer

Spermatogenesis gives 4 sperms per spermatocyte, oogenesis gives 1 ovum + polar bodies; spermatogenesis is continuous from puberty, oogenesis starts in the foetus and pauses in prophase I.

6.Why is lactational amenorrhoea a natural contraceptive? Give one limitation.

Show answer

Intense breast-feeding suppresses ovulation and menstruation. It works only up to about 6 months after birth, and only with full breast-feeding.

7.16% of a population show a recessive trait. Find q and the frequency of heterozygotes.

Show answer

q² = 0.16 ⇒ q = 0.4, p = 0.6 ⇒ 2pq = 0.48.

8.What is a test cross? Why is it done?

Show answer

Crossing an organism with a dominant phenotype with a homozygous recessive. A 1 : 1 ratio shows the unknown is heterozygous; all dominant shows it is homozygous.

9.Why is colour blindness more common in men?

Show answer

It is X-linked recessive. Men (XY) need only one recessive allele to be affected; women need two, so they are usually carriers.

10.What did the Hershey–Chase experiment prove and how?

Show answer

DNA is the genetic material: ³²P-labelled DNA entered bacteria, while ³⁵S-labelled protein stayed in the viral coats.

11.Write the mRNA transcribed from the template strand 3′-TACGGA-5′ and the coding strand.

Show answer

mRNA 5′-AUGCCU-3′; coding strand 5′-ATGCCT-3′.

12.Distinguish homologous and analogous organs with one example each.

Show answer

Homologous: same origin, different function (forelimbs of whale and bat). Analogous: different origin, same function (wings of butterfly and bird).

13.Differentiate innate and acquired immunity.

Show answer

Innate: present at birth, non-specific, no memory (skin, phagocytes). Acquired: develops after exposure, specific, has memory (antibodies, T cells).

14.Why do malarial chills and fever recur every 3–4 days?

Show answer

Plasmodium multiplies in RBCs; when they burst together every 3–4 days, haemozoin is released, causing the chill and fever.

15.Name the source and use of statins and streptokinase.

Show answer

Statins — Monascus purpureus, lower blood cholesterol. Streptokinase — Streptococcus, dissolves clots in heart-attack patients.

16.What is BOD? Why is it measured in sewage treatment?

Show answer

The oxygen needed by bacteria to oxidise the organic matter in a litre of water. A high BOD means high pollution; it shows how well the water has been treated.

17.What are sticky ends? Why are they useful?

Show answer

Single-stranded overhangs left when a restriction enzyme cuts away from the centre of a palindrome. They pair with complementary ends cut by the same enzyme, so DNA ligase can join vector and insert.

18.What is insertional inactivation?

Show answer

Inserting foreign DNA into a selectable marker gene (e.g. tetᴿ at BamHI or β-galactosidase) inactivates it, so recombinants can be told apart (tet-sensitive or white colonies).

19.Why is gene therapy for ADA deficiency not a permanent cure?

Show answer

The corrected lymphocytes are not immortal, so periodic infusions are needed. A permanent cure needs the gene introduced into bone-marrow or early embryonic stem cells.

20.Write the logistic growth equation. What does K represent?

Show answer

dN/dt = rN(K − N)/K. K is the carrying capacity — the maximum population the environment can support.

3-mark questions

Short answer.

1.Describe three outbreeding devices in flowering plants.

Show answer

Dichogamy (pollen release and stigma receptivity at different times); herkogamy/position of anther and stigma; self-incompatibility (genetic block to self-pollen germination); also unisexual flowers (dioecy).

2.What is double fertilisation? Name its products.

Show answer

One male gamete fuses with the egg (syngamy) → zygote (2n); the other fuses with the two polar nuclei (triple fusion) → primary endosperm nucleus (3n), which forms the endosperm.

3.Describe the phases of the menstrual cycle.

Show answer

Menstrual (days 1–5, endometrium breaks down); follicular (FSH/LH rise, follicle grows, estrogen rebuilds endometrium); ovulation (~day 14, LH surge); luteal (corpus luteum secretes progesterone).

4.Give the functions of the placenta and the hormones it secretes.

Show answer

Exchange of O₂, nutrients, CO₂ and wastes between mother and foetus; acts as an endocrine gland secreting hCG, hPL, estrogens and progestogens.

5.A woman has blocked fallopian tubes and her husband a very low sperm count. Suggest ART and justify.

Show answer

IVF–ET bypasses the tubes (fertilisation in vitro, embryo placed in the uterus); ICSI injects one sperm directly into the ovum, overcoming low count/motility.

6.A plant heterozygous for seed shape and colour is selfed. Give the parental genotype, F2 phenotypic ratio, and the number with both dominant traits out of 160.

Show answer

RrYy; 9 : 3 : 3 : 1; 9/16 × 160 = 90.

7.A mother with blood group A and father with B have a child with O. Explain with genotypes.

Show answer

Parents must be Iᴬi and Iᴮi. Each passes i with probability ½, so ii (O) occurs with probability ¼. Other children: AB, A, B.

8.P–Q = 20, Q–R = 12 and P–R = 8 map units. Find the gene order and draw the map.

Show answer

8 + 12 = 20 ⇒ R lies between: P — 8 — R — 12 — Q.

9.E. coli grown in ¹⁵N is moved to ¹⁴N. Describe the DNA after 1 and 2 generations and the conclusion.

Show answer

Generation 1: all hybrid (¹⁵N/¹⁴N). Generation 2: half hybrid, half light. Conclusion: replication is semi-conservative (Meselson and Stahl).

10.Explain the working of the lac operon when lactose is present.

Show answer

Lactose (inducer) binds the repressor from the i gene, which then cannot bind the operator; RNA polymerase transcribes z, y, a genes making β-galactosidase, permease and transacetylase.

11.Describe the processing of hnRNA into mRNA in eukaryotes.

Show answer

Splicing (introns removed, exons joined), capping (methyl guanosine triphosphate added at 5′ end) and tailing (poly-A tail at 3′ end).

12.State the Hardy–Weinberg principle and three factors that disturb it.

Show answer

Allele frequencies stay constant in a large, randomly mating population with no evolutionary forces. Disturbing factors: gene flow, genetic drift, mutation, recombination, natural selection.

13.Explain industrial melanism as an example of natural selection.

Show answer

Before industrialisation, white moths on lichen-covered trees escaped predators; after soot darkened trees, dark moths were camouflaged and increased. Selection acted on existing variation.

14.How does HIV multiply and cause immunodeficiency?

Show answer

It enters macrophages; reverse transcriptase makes viral DNA, which integrates into host DNA; macrophages produce virus; HIV attacks helper T cells, whose number falls, leading to opportunistic infections.

15.Differentiate benign and malignant tumours and name two causes of cancer.

Show answer

Benign stays localised; malignant grows fast, invades and spreads by metastasis. Causes: ionising/UV radiation, chemical carcinogens (tobacco), oncogenic viruses.

16.Describe the secondary treatment of sewage.

Show answer

Primary effluent is aerated; flocs of aerobic microbes consume organic matter and lower BOD; it settles as activated sludge — part returned as inoculum, rest digested anaerobically to give biogas.

17.Describe the three steps of PCR.

Show answer

Denaturation (~94 °C, strands separate); annealing (primers bind); extension (Taq polymerase adds nucleotides at ~72 °C). Repeating doubles the DNA each cycle.

18.How does Bt cotton resist bollworms?

Show answer

cry genes (cryIAc, cryIIAb) make an inactive protoxin; in the alkaline insect gut it becomes active, binds midgut cells, makes pores, and the cells swell and burst, killing the larva.

19.How was RNAi used to protect tobacco from nematodes?

Show answer

Nematode-specific genes were introduced with Agrobacterium so the plant made sense and antisense RNA; their dsRNA silenced the nematode's mRNA, so the parasite could not survive.

20.Complete: mutualism (+,+), competition (?), predation (?), commensalism (?), amensalism (?). Give one example of commensalism.

Show answer

Competition (−,−); predation (+,−); commensalism (+,0); amensalism (−,0). Example: orchid growing on a mango branch.

4-mark case-based questions

Read the situation; answer all parts.

1.A student must cross two tomato varieties. (i) Why emasculate? (ii) Why bag the flower? (iii) When is pollen dusted? (iv) Is emasculation needed for a unisexual female flower?

Show answer

(i) to prevent self-pollination (ii) to prevent contamination with unwanted pollen (iii) when the stigma becomes receptive (iv) no, only bagging.

2.Viola bears both open and closed flowers. (i) Name the two types. (ii) Which pollination occurs in closed flowers? (iii) One advantage of closed flowers? (iv) Which type benefits the species more?

Show answer

(i) chasmogamous and cleistogamous (ii) autogamy (iii) assured seed set even without pollinators (iv) chasmogamous — cross-pollination gives variation.

3.In Ophrys, a petal looks like a female bee. (i) What does the male bee do? (ii) Name this. (iii) What interaction is it? (iv) Why must the flower and bee co-evolve?

Show answer

(i) attempts to mate and picks up pollen (ii) pseudocopulation / sexual deceit (iii) mutualism (iv) if the female bee's pattern changes, the flower must change too or pollination fails.

4.A couple: wife's ovaries are normal but tubes blocked; husband's sperm motility is very poor. (i) Suitable ART? (ii) Where does fertilisation occur? (iii) How is the embryo transferred? (iv) Why ICSI for the husband?

Show answer

(i) IVF–ET with ICSI (ii) in the lab (in vitro) (iii) into the uterus (IUT, more than 8 blastomeres) (iv) motility isn't needed — the sperm is injected directly.

5.A hormone graph shows estrogen rising, then a sharp LH peak mid-cycle, then progesterone dominating. (i) What does the LH peak cause? (ii) Which structure secretes progesterone? (iii) What happens if fertilisation does not occur? (iv) Why doesn't menstruation start right after ovulation?

Show answer

(i) ovulation (ii) corpus luteum (iii) it degenerates, progesterone falls, endometrium breaks down (iv) the corpus luteum keeps progesterone high for about two weeks.

6.200 primary oocytes complete oogenesis. (i) How many ova? (ii) How many polar bodies (if the first divides)? (iii) Why is division unequal? (iv) When is meiosis II completed?

Show answer

(i) 200 (ii) 600 (iii) to keep cytoplasm (food) in the ovum (iv) after a sperm enters.

7.A disorder appears in every generation, affects both sexes, and an affected child usually has an affected parent. (i) Inheritance pattern? (ii) Can a heterozygous parent pass it on? (iii) Probability for a child of Aa × aa? (iv) Example.

Show answer

(i) autosomal dominant (ii) yes (iii) ½ (iv) myotonic dystrophy.

8.A carrier woman for haemophilia marries a normal man. (i) Genotypes? (ii) Chance of an affected son? (iii) Chance of a carrier daughter? (iv) Why are affected women rare?

Show answer

(i) XᴴXʰ × XᴴY (ii) ½ of sons (iii) ½ of daughters (iv) they need two copies — father must be affected and mother a carrier.

9.Sickle-cell anaemia. (i) Type of mutation? (ii) The change in the gene/protein? (iii) Genotype of a patient? (iv) When do RBCs sickle?

Show answer

(i) point mutation (substitution) (ii) GAG → GUG, glutamic acid → valine at position 6 of β-globin (iii) HbˢHbˢ (iv) under low oxygen.

10.The Human Genome Project. (i) Approximate size of the genome? (ii) What are ESTs? (iii) Which chromosome has the most genes? (iv) One application.

Show answer

(i) about 3164.7 million bp (ii) expressed sequence tags — genes expressed as RNA (iii) chromosome 1 (iv) diagnosing/treating genetic disorders.

11.Blood at a crime scene is compared with suspects. (i) Which sequences are compared? (ii) Why are they useful? (iii) Order the steps. (iv) One other use.

Show answer

(i) VNTRs (satellite DNA) (ii) highly variable between individuals but inherited (iii) isolate DNA → restriction digest → gel electrophoresis → blotting → hybridise with VNTR probe → autoradiography (iv) paternity testing.

12.A bacterial population becomes antibiotic-resistant after repeated treatment. (i) What mechanism is shown? (ii) Did the antibiotic cause the mutation? (iii) What is fitness here? (iv) Type of selection?

Show answer

(i) natural selection (ii) no, resistant variants already existed (iii) ability to survive and reproduce (iv) directional.

13.A child is vaccinated against hepatitis B. (i) Type of immunity? (ii) Which cells give a fast response later? (iii) What is the second response called? (iv) Why are recombinant vaccines safer?

Show answer

(i) active, artificially acquired (ii) memory B and T cells (iii) secondary/anamnestic response (iv) they contain only the antigen, not the pathogen.

14.A teenager under peer pressure starts using drugs. (i) A drug from the poppy? (ii) Effect of cocaine? (iii) Two warning signs? (iv) Two preventive measures?

Show answer

(i) heroin/morphine (ii) interferes with dopamine transport (iii) falling grades, isolation, change in habits (iv) avoid undue peer pressure, counselling, help from parents and teachers.

15.An organic plot (no pesticide) had 42 pests, 24 beneficial insects and 7% damage; a chemical plot had 15 pests, 6 beneficial insects and 5% damage. (i) Is zero pests the goal? (ii) Long-term risk of the chemical plot? (iii) An eco-friendly agent? (iv) Why is NPV preferred?

Show answer

(i) no — keep pests below damaging levels while conserving natural enemies (ii) loss of beneficial insects causes pest resurgence and resistance (iii) Trichoderma, ladybirds, Bt (iv) species-specific, no effect on non-target organisms.

16.A village builds a gobar gas plant. (i) Which microbes produce biogas? (ii) Conditions? (iii) Why add cattle dung? (iv) Use of the spent slurry?

Show answer

(i) methanogens (e.g. Methanobacterium) (ii) anaerobic (iii) it contains methanogens (iv) as manure/fertiliser.

17.Foreign DNA is inserted at the BamHI site of pBR322 and E. coli is transformed. (i) Which gene is inactivated? (ii) How are recombinants identified? (iii) Role of ori? (iv) Why only one BamHI site?

Show answer

(i) tetᴿ (ii) grow on ampicillin but not on tetracycline (replica plating) (iii) starts replication and controls copy number (iv) multiple sites would cut the vector into pieces.

18.Humulin is made by rDNA technology. (i) Which chain is removed from pro-insulin? (ii) How did Eli Lilly make it? (iii) How are chains joined? (iv) Advantage over animal insulin?

Show answer

(i) the C peptide (ii) made chains A and B separately in E. coli (iii) disulphide bonds (iv) no allergic reaction.

19.DNA fragments are separated on an agarose gel. (i) Towards which electrode do they move and why? (ii) Which fragments move farthest? (iii) How are bands seen? (iv) What is elution?

Show answer

(i) anode — DNA is negatively charged (ii) the smallest (iii) ethidium bromide stain under UV (iv) cutting out bands and extracting the DNA.

20.A deer population grows fast at first, then levels off. (i) Name the curve. (ii) Equation? (iii) Why does growth slow? (iv) Under what conditions would growth be exponential?

Show answer

(i) logistic (sigmoid) (ii) dN/dt = rN(K − N)/K (iii) limited resources, competition — nearing carrying capacity (iv) unlimited resources.

5-mark questions

Long answer. Key points are given; full answers are in the notes.

1.Describe microsporogenesis and draw the TS of a mature anther. State the functions of the tapetum and endothecium.

Show answer

MMC (2n) → meiosis → tetrad → pollen. Wall: epidermis, endothecium (dehiscence), middle layers, tapetum (nourishes pollen). Notes

2.Describe megasporogenesis and the development of the embryo sac. Draw a labelled mature embryo sac.

Show answer

MMC meiosis → 4 megaspores, 1 functional (monosporic); 3 mitotic free-nuclear divisions → 8 nuclei, 7 cells. Notes

3.Explain pollination types, outbreeding devices and the steps of artificial hybridisation.

Show answer

Autogamy, geitonogamy, xenogamy; dichogamy, self-incompatibility, unisexual flowers; emasculation, bagging, pollination, rebagging.

4.Describe spermatogenesis and its hormonal control. Draw a labelled sperm.

Show answer

Spermatogonia → primary spermatocytes → meiosis → spermatids → spermiogenesis → sperms; GnRH → FSH (Sertoli) and LH (Leydig). Sperm: head with acrosome, middle piece with mitochondria, tail.

5.Describe oogenesis and relate it to the menstrual cycle.

Show answer

Oogonia in the foetus → primary oocytes arrested in prophase I → at puberty meiosis I → secondary oocyte + polar body → ovulation at LH surge → meiosis II after sperm entry.

6.Trace the events from fertilisation to implantation and describe parturition.

Show answer

Fertilisation in ampulla → cleavage → morula → blastocyst (trophoblast + inner cell mass) → implantation. Parturition: foetal ejection reflex → oxytocin → stronger contractions (positive feedback).

7.Classify contraceptive methods with examples and their mode of action.

Show answer

Natural, barrier, IUDs (non-medicated, copper, hormonal), pills (Saheli), injectables/implants, surgical (vasectomy, tubectomy). Notes

8.Explain Mendel's dihybrid cross with a Punnett square and state the laws it shows.

Show answer

RRYY × rryy → F1 RrYy → F2 9 : 3 : 3 : 1. Laws of segregation and independent assortment. Notes

9.Explain the types of sex determination and sex-linked inheritance with an example.

Show answer

XX–XY, XX–XO, ZW–ZZ, haplodiploidy; haemophilia/colour blindness passed from carrier mothers to half of sons.

10.Describe Down's, Turner's and Klinefelter's syndromes and two Mendelian disorders.

Show answer

Trisomy 21; 45 XO; 47 XXY. Mendelian: sickle-cell anaemia, phenylketonuria, thalassaemia, haemophilia.

11.Describe DNA replication with a labelled replication fork.

Show answer

Semi-conservative; helicase unwinds; DNA polymerase 5′→3′; leading strand continuous, lagging in Okazaki fragments joined by ligase; starts at ori. Notes

12.Describe transcription in bacteria and the processing of hnRNA in eukaryotes.

Show answer

RNA polymerase with σ factor at promoter → elongation → ρ factor at terminator. Eukaryotes: splicing, capping, tailing.

13.State the features of the genetic code and describe translation.

Show answer

Triplet, degenerate, unambiguous, nearly universal, AUG start, 3 stops; charging of tRNA, initiation at AUG, elongation, termination.

14.Explain Darwin's theory and the factors affecting Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium.

Show answer

Overproduction, variation, struggle, survival of the fittest, speciation; gene flow, drift, mutation, recombination, selection.

15.Explain innate and acquired immunity, antibody structure, and vaccination.

Show answer

Four barriers; humoral (B cells) and cell-mediated (T cells); antibody H₂L₂; memory cells in vaccination.

16.Describe the life cycle of Plasmodium and how AIDS weakens immunity.

Show answer

Sporozoites → liver → RBCs → haemozoin → gametocytes → mosquito gut → salivary glands. HIV kills helper T cells. Notes

17.Describe the role of microbes in industry and in sewage treatment.

Show answer

Fermented beverages, organic acids, enzymes, antibiotics, statins, streptokinase, cyclosporin A; primary and secondary sewage treatment, flocs, activated sludge.

18.Describe the steps of recombinant DNA technology from isolating DNA to obtaining the product.

Show answer

Isolation → restriction digestion → amplification (PCR) → ligation into vector → transformation (competent cells) → selection → culture in bioreactor → downstream processing.

19.Explain Bt crops, RNAi, recombinant insulin and gene therapy as applications of biotechnology.

Show answer

Cry protoxin; dsRNA silencing in Meloidogyne; chains A and B made separately; ADA gene via retrovirus into lymphocytes. Notes

20.Explain exponential and logistic growth, and describe population interactions with examples.

Show answer

dN/dt = rN vs rN(K − N)/K; mutualism, competition (Gause), predation, parasitism, commensalism, amensalism. Notes
