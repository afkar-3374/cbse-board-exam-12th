One rule for the whole chapter (sign convention): measure every distance from the pole (mirror) or optical centre (lens). Along the direction of the incoming light is positive, against it is negative. Heights above the axis are positive, below are negative. Light is always drawn coming from the left, so the object distance u is negative.

1. Reflection by spherical mirrors

A spherical mirror is a piece cut from a hollow sphere. A concave mirror curves in like a cave and brings parallel rays together at the focus F. A convex mirror bulges out and spreads them, so they only seem to come from F behind the mirror.

  • Pole P: centre of the mirror. C: centre of the sphere. R: radius of curvature.
  • Focus is halfway between P and C.
f = R/2   |   1/v + 1/u = 1/f   |   m = h′/h = −v/u
  1. A ray parallel to the axis reflects through F (concave) or appears to come from F (convex).
  2. A ray hitting the pole reflects at an equal angle on the other side of the axis.
  3. Where the two reflected rays meet is the image. If they only meet when extended backwards (dashed), the image is virtual.
Object position (concave mirror)Image
Beyond CBetween F and C, real, inverted, smaller
At CAt C, real, inverted, same size
Between C and FBeyond C, real, inverted, larger
At FAt infinity
Between F and PBehind mirror, virtual, erect, larger

A convex mirror always gives a virtual, erect, smaller image between P and F. That is why it gives a wide field of view as a rear-view mirror.

Topic 1 over: test yourself

Mirrors, mirror equation, magnification

NCERT 9.1A small candle, 2.5 cm in size, is placed 27 cm in front of a concave mirror of radius of curvature 36 cm. At what distance from the mirror should a screen be placed to get a sharp image? Describe the nature and size of the image.

Show answer

f = −18 cm, u = −27 cm. 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = −1/18 + 1/27 = −1/54, so v = −54 cm: screen 54 cm in front of the mirror.

m = −v/u = −2, so image height = −5 cm: real, inverted, 5 cm tall. If the candle moves closer, the screen must move farther away. Closer than 18 cm the image becomes virtual and cannot be caught on a screen.

NCERT 9.2A 4.5 cm needle is placed 12 cm away from a convex mirror of focal length 15 cm. Give the location of the image and the magnification.

Show answer

u = −12 cm, f = +15 cm. 1/v = 1/15 + 1/12 = 9/60, so v = +6.7 cm (behind the mirror).

m = −v/u = +0.56, image height = 2.5 cm: virtual, erect, diminished. As the needle moves away, the image moves towards F and keeps shrinking.

Board3 MAn object is placed 20 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 15 cm. Find the position, nature and magnification of the image.

Show answer

u = −20, f = −15. 1/v = −1/15 + 1/20 = −1/60, so v = −60 cm. m = −v/u = −3.

Real, inverted, three times magnified, 60 cm in front of the mirror. (Set u = 20, f = 15 in the diagram above to see it.)

Board2 MUse the mirror equation to show that an object placed between f and 2f of a concave mirror produces a real image beyond 2f. (Also NCERT 9.15 a)

Show answer

For a concave mirror f < 0 and u < 0, with 2f < u < f.

So 1/2f > 1/u > 1/f, which gives −1/2f < −1/u < −1/f. Adding 1/f: 1/f − 1/2f < 1/v < 0, i.e. 1/2f < 1/v < 0.

Hence v is negative (real image) and |v| > |2f|: the image lies beyond 2f.

Board1 MWhy is a convex mirror preferred as a rear-view mirror in vehicles?

Show answer

It always forms an erect, diminished image, so it covers a much wider field of view than a plane mirror of the same size.

2. Refraction and Snell's law

Light slows down in a denser medium. When it enters at an angle, one side of the beam slows first, so the ray bends towards the normal. Going from denser to rarer it speeds up and bends away from the normal.

n = c/v   |   n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r   |   n₂₁ = n₂/n₁ = v₁/v₂ = λ₁/λ₂

Frequency never changes on refraction. Speed and wavelength change.

Two results you must know

Apparent depth

apparent depth = real depth / n

A pool looks shallower than it is. Shift = t (1 − 1/n) for a slab of thickness t.

Glass slab

The emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray, only shifted sideways (lateral shift). No deviation.

Topic 2 over: test yourself

Snell's law, refractive index, apparent depth

NCERT 9.3A tank is filled with water to a height of 12.5 cm. The apparent depth of a needle lying at the bottom is measured by a microscope to be 9.4 cm. What is the refractive index of water? If water is replaced by a liquid of refractive index 1.63 up to the same height, by what distance would the microscope have to be moved to focus on the needle again?

Show answer

n = real depth / apparent depth = 12.5 / 9.4 = 1.33.

New apparent depth = 12.5 / 1.63 = 7.67 cm. The microscope must be moved up by 9.4 − 7.67 = 1.73 cm.

NCERT 9.16A small pin fixed on a table top is viewed from above from a distance of 50 cm. By what distance would the pin appear to be raised if it is viewed through a 15 cm thick glass slab (n = 1.5) held parallel to the table? Does the answer depend on the location of the slab?

Show answer

Shift = t (1 − 1/n) = 15 × (1 − 1/1.5) = 5 cm. It does not depend on where the slab is placed (for small angles of incidence).

Board1 MWhen light travels from air into glass, which of these change: frequency, wavelength, speed?

Show answer

Speed and wavelength both decrease by the factor n. Frequency stays the same, because it is fixed by the source.

Board2 MThe refractive index of glass is 1.5. Find the speed of light in glass. If light of wavelength 600 nm in air enters the glass, what is its wavelength inside?

Show answer

v = c/n = 3 × 10⁸ / 1.5 = 2 × 10⁸ m/s. λ′ = λ/n = 600/1.5 = 400 nm.

3. Total internal reflection

Send light from a denser medium towards a rarer one and keep increasing the angle. The refracted ray bends further and further from the normal until it skims the surface. That angle of incidence is the critical angle ic. Go beyond it and no light gets out at all: everything is reflected back.

sin ic = nrarer/ndenser = 1/n (rarer medium is air)
Two conditions for TIR: (1) light must travel from denser to rarer medium, (2) angle of incidence must be greater than the critical angle.

Where you see it

Optical fibre

A glass core surrounded by cladding of lower refractive index. Light hits the core wall at more than ic, so it bounces along the fibre by repeated TIR with almost no loss, even round bends.

Totally reflecting prisms

A 45°–90°–45° glass prism (ic ≈ 42°) turns light by 90° or 180°, because light meets the slanted face at 45° > ic. Needs n > √2.

Diamond sparkles because its critical angle is only about 24°, so light is trapped by many internal reflections. Mirage in a desert is the same effect in layers of hot air.

Topic 3 over: test yourself

Critical angle, TIR, optical fibre

NCERT 9.5A small bulb is placed at the bottom of a tank containing water to a depth of 80 cm. What is the area of the surface of water through which light from the bulb can emerge? Refractive index of water is 1.33. (Treat the bulb as a point source.)

Show answer

Light escapes only inside a cone of half-angle ic. sin ic = 1/1.33, so ic ≈ 48.75°.

Radius of the bright circle r = h tan ic = 0.80 × 1.14 = 0.91 m. Area = πr² ≈ 2.6 m².

Board2 MState the conditions for total internal reflection. Calculate the critical angle for a glass–air surface (n = 1.5).

Show answer

Light must go from the denser to the rarer medium, and the angle of incidence must exceed the critical angle.

sin ic = 1/1.5 = 0.667, so ic ≈ 41.8°.

Board3 MWith a diagram, explain how an optical fibre transmits light. Why is the cladding made of a material of lower refractive index than the core?

Show answer

Light entering one end strikes the core–cladding boundary at an angle greater than the critical angle and is totally internally reflected. This repeats along the length, so the light comes out at the other end with very little loss.

TIR needs light to go from denser to rarer medium, so the cladding must have the lower refractive index.

Board2 MA ray falls normally on one of the short faces of a right-angled isosceles glass prism. What is the minimum refractive index of the glass for the ray to be totally reflected at the hypotenuse?

Show answer

The ray meets the hypotenuse at 45°. For TIR, 45° ≥ ic, so sin 45° ≥ 1/n, giving n ≥ √2 ≈ 1.414.

4. Refraction at a spherical surface, lenses

A lens is just two curved surfaces. So first learn what one curved surface does, then apply it twice.

n₂/v − n₁/u = (n₂ − n₁)/R

Apply this at the first surface of a lens, use that image as the object for the second surface, add the two equations, and you get the lens maker's formula:

1/f = (n₂₁ − 1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂)   |   1/v − 1/u = 1/f   |   m = v/u

Image formation by a lens

  1. A ray parallel to the axis passes through the second focus F (convex) or appears to come from the first focus (concave).
  2. A ray through the optical centre goes straight, undeviated.
  3. Their meeting point is the image.

Power and combination

P = 1/f (f in metres, unit dioptre D)   |   lenses in contact: 1/f = 1/f₁ + 1/f₂, P = P₁ + P₂, m = m₁m₂

Convex lens: positive power. Concave lens: negative power.

Spherical surface, lens maker's formula, thin lens, power

NCERT 9.7Double-convex lenses are to be manufactured from a glass of refractive index 1.55, with both faces of the same radius of curvature. What is the radius of curvature required if the focal length is to be 20 cm?

Show answer

1/f = (n − 1)(2/R), so R = 2(n − 1) f = 2 × 0.55 × 20 = 22 cm. (Check it in the lens maker above: n = 1.55, R₁ = R₂ = 22.)

NCERT 9.8A beam of light converges at a point P. A lens is placed in the path of the convergent beam 12 cm from P. At what point does the beam converge if the lens is (a) a convex lens of focal length 20 cm, (b) a concave lens of focal length 16 cm?

Show answer

P acts as a virtual object, so u = +12 cm.

(a) 1/v = 1/20 + 1/12 = 8/60, v = 7.5 cm beyond the lens.

(b) 1/v = −1/16 + 1/12 = 1/48, v = 48 cm beyond the lens.

NCERT 9.9An object of size 3.0 cm is placed 14 cm in front of a concave lens of focal length 21 cm. Describe the image produced by the lens.

Show answer

u = −14, f = −21. 1/v = −1/21 − 1/14 = −5/42, so v = −8.4 cm. m = v/u = 0.6, image size = 1.8 cm.

Virtual, erect, diminished, on the same side as the object. Moving the object away pushes the image towards the focus but never beyond it.

NCERT 9.10What is the focal length of a convex lens of focal length 30 cm in contact with a concave lens of focal length 20 cm? Is the system converging or diverging?

Show answer

1/f = 1/30 − 1/20 = −1/60, so f = −60 cm. The combination is a diverging lens.

Board5 MDerive the relation n₂/v − n₁/u = (n₂ − n₁)/R for refraction at a convex spherical surface, and use it to obtain the lens maker's formula.

Show answer

Surface: for small angles, i = ∠NOM + ∠NCM and r = ∠NCM − ∠NIM (exterior angle theorem). Snell's law for small angles gives n₁ i = n₂ r. Writing each angle as (height MN)/(distance) and applying the sign convention (OM = −u, MI = +v, MC = +R) gives n₂/v − n₁/u = (n₂ − n₁)/R.

Lens: first surface (object at infinity side): n₂/v₁ − n₁/u = (n₂ − n₁)/R₁. Second surface, with the first image as object: n₁/v − n₂/v₁ = (n₁ − n₂)/R₂. Add them: n₁(1/v − 1/u) = (n₂ − n₁)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂). Put u = ∞, v = f: 1/f = (n₂₁ − 1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂).

Board2 MA convex lens of glass (n = 1.5) is dipped in turn in liquids of refractive index (a) 1.33, (b) 1.5, (c) 1.65. How does it behave in each?

Show answer

1/f ∝ (nglass/nliquid − 1).

(a) Still converging but weaker: focal length becomes about 4 times. (b) Bracket is zero, f = ∞: acts like a plane glass sheet and becomes invisible. (c) Bracket is negative: it behaves as a diverging lens. Try the "surrounding medium" option in the lens maker above.

5. Refraction through a prism

A prism bends a ray twice, both times towards its base. The total turning is the angle of deviation δ.

r₁ + r₂ = A   |   δ = i + e − A

As you increase i from a small value, δ first falls, reaches a minimum, then rises. At minimum deviation the path is symmetric: i = e, r₁ = r₂ = A/2, and the ray inside is parallel to the base.

n = sin[(A + δm)/2] / sin(A/2)   |   thin prism: δ = (n − 1)A

Topic 5 over: test yourself

Prism, deviation, minimum deviation

NCERT 9.6A prism is made of glass of unknown refractive index. A parallel beam of light is incident on a face of the prism. The angle of minimum deviation is measured to be 40°. What is the refractive index of the material of the prism? The refracting angle of the prism is 60°. If the prism is placed in water (n = 1.33), predict the new angle of minimum deviation.

Show answer

n = sin[(60° + 40°)/2] / sin 30° = sin 50° / 0.5 = 1.53.

In water, relative index = 1.53/1.33 = 1.15. sin[(60° + δ′)/2] = 1.15 × 0.5 = 0.576, so (60° + δ′)/2 ≈ 35.2° and δ′ ≈ 10°.

NCERT 9.21At what angle should a ray of light be incident on the face of a prism of refracting angle 60° so that it just suffers total internal reflection at the other face? The refractive index of the material of the prism is 1.524.

Show answer

At the second face r₂ = ic, with sin ic = 1/1.524, so ic ≈ 41°. Then r₁ = A − r₂ = 19°.

sin i = 1.524 × sin 19° ≈ 0.496, so i ≈ 30°. (In the diagram above set A = 60, n = 1.52 and lower i towards 30.)

Board3 MDraw the graph of angle of deviation against angle of incidence for a prism. Derive the expression for the refractive index of the prism in terms of A and δm.

Show answer

The graph is a U-shaped curve with a single minimum δm (see the small graph in the diagram above).

In the quadrilateral formed by the two normals, r₁ + r₂ = A, and δ = (i − r₁) + (e − r₂) = i + e − A. At minimum deviation i = e and r₁ = r₂ = r, so r = A/2 and i = (A + δm)/2. Snell's law: n = sin i / sin r = sin[(A + δm)/2] / sin(A/2).

Board2 MA prism of angle 60° is made of glass of refractive index √2. Find the angle of minimum deviation and the corresponding angle of incidence.

Show answer

sin[(60° + δm)/2] = √2 × sin 30° = 0.707, so (60° + δm)/2 = 45°. δm = 30° and i = 45°.

6. Microscope and telescope

Your eye judges size by the angle an object makes at it. Optical instruments make that angle bigger. Least distance of distinct vision D = 25 cm.

Simple microscope (magnifying glass)

A convex lens of small focal length with the object placed within f. It gives a virtual, erect, enlarged image.

image at near point: m = 1 + D/f   |   image at infinity (relaxed eye): m = D/f

Compound microscope

Two convex lenses. The objective (very small fo) makes a real, inverted, enlarged image. The eyepiece then acts as a magnifying glass on that image.

m = mo × me ≈ (L/fo)(D/fe)   (final image at infinity, L = tube length)

Astronomical telescope

Objective of large focal length and large aperture collects light from a distant object and forms a real image at its focus. The eyepiece of small focal length magnifies it. In normal adjustment the final image is at infinity.

m = β/α = fo/fe   |   tube length L = fo + fe
Reflecting telescope (mirror objective): no chromatic aberration, a parabolic mirror removes spherical aberration, a mirror is lighter and easier to support than a huge lens, and it can be made very large to gather more light.

Topic 6 over: test yourself

Microscopes and telescopes

NCERT 9.11A compound microscope consists of an objective lens of focal length 2.0 cm and an eyepiece of focal length 6.25 cm separated by a distance of 15 cm. How far from the objective should an object be placed to obtain the final image at (a) the least distance of distinct vision (25 cm), (b) infinity? What is the magnifying power in each case?

Show answer

(a) Eyepiece: ve = −25, so 1/ue = −1/25 − 1/6.25 = −1/5, ue = −5 cm. So vo = 15 − 5 = 10 cm. 1/uo = 1/10 − 1/2 = −2/5, uo = −2.5 cm. m = (10/2.5)(1 + 25/6.25) = 4 × 5 = 20.

(b) ue = 6.25 cm, so vo = 8.75 cm. 1/uo = 1/8.75 − 1/2, uo = −2.59 cm. m = (8.75/2.59)(25/6.25) = 13.5.

NCERT 9.13A small telescope has an objective lens of focal length 144 cm and an eyepiece of focal length 6.0 cm. What is the magnifying power of the telescope? What is the separation between the objective and the eyepiece?

Show answer

m = fo/fe = 144/6 = 24. Separation = fo + fe = 150 cm.

NCERT 9.27A small telescope has an objective lens of focal length 140 cm and an eyepiece of focal length 5.0 cm. What is the magnifying power for viewing distant objects when (a) the telescope is in normal adjustment, (b) the final image is formed at the least distance of distinct vision (25 cm)?

Show answer

(a) m = 140/5 = 28. (b) m = (fo/fe)(1 + fe/D) = 28 × (1 + 5/25) = 33.6.

Board3 MDraw a ray diagram of an astronomical telescope in normal adjustment and write the expression for its magnifying power. Give two advantages of a reflecting telescope over a refracting one.

Show answer

Diagram: as in the telescope above, with parallel rays in and parallel rays out. m = fo/fe, length = fo + fe.

Advantages of a reflecting telescope: no chromatic aberration; spherical aberration removed by a parabolic mirror; a large mirror is easier to make and support, so brighter images and better resolving power.

Board2 MWhy should the objective of a telescope have a large focal length and large aperture, while both lenses of a compound microscope have small focal lengths?

Show answer

Telescope: m = fo/fe, so a large fo gives high magnification; a large aperture gathers more light and gives better resolving power.

Microscope: m ≈ (L/fo)(D/fe), so both focal lengths must be small for high magnification.
