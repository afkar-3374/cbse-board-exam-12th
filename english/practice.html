English · Practice Questions

1-mark questions

Objective items of the kind asked in extract-based questions (Q7–Q9).

1.Identify the poetic device in "pale as a late winter's moon" (My Mother at Sixty-six).

Show answer

Simile.

2.What does the repetition in "smile and smile and smile" convey?

Show answer

The poet's effort to hide her fear and pain behind a brave face.

3.In Keeping Quiet, up to what number does the poet ask us to count?

Show answer

Twelve.

4.Which device is used in "Perhaps the Earth can teach us"?

Show answer

Personification.

5.In A Thing of Beauty, what does "bower" mean?

Show answer

A shady, quiet shelter (a place of rest and peace).

6.What do the owners of the roadside stand want from passing city people?

Show answer

Some city money (a fair trade), not charity.

7.Why are Aunt Jennifer's fingers "fluttering"?

Show answer

Out of fear and nervousness — the burden of an oppressive marriage.

8.What does "the massive weight of Uncle's wedding band" symbolise?

Show answer

The oppression and burden of marriage under a dominating husband.

9.What order came from Berlin in The Last Lesson?

Show answer

Only German was to be taught in the schools of Alsace and Lorraine.

10.What does Saheb's full name, Saheb-e-Alam, mean?

Show answer

Lord of the universe — ironic, given his poverty.

11.Where was Douglas thrown into deep water as a boy?

Show answer

In the YMCA swimming pool.

12.How did the peddler sign the note he left with the rattrap?

Show answer

Captain von Stahle.

13.What refund did Gandhi accept from the landlords?

Show answer

25 per cent.

14.Which studio does Poets and Pancakes describe?

Show answer

Gemini Studios, Madras.

15.How does Umberto Eco describe himself?

Show answer

A professor (scholar) who writes novels on Sundays.

16.Who is Sophie's hero in Going Places?

Show answer

Danny Casey, the young Irish football player.

17.Where does Charley's third level lead?

Show answer

To Galesburg, Illinois, in 1894.

18.What finally killed the Tiger King?

Show answer

A sliver of the wooden toy tiger that pricked his hand and caused an infection.

19.Name the ship on which Tishani Doshi travelled to Antarctica.

Show answer

The Akademik Shokalskiy (a Russian research vessel).

20.What had happened to Derry's face?

Show answer

One side was burnt by acid.

2-mark questions

Answer in 40–50 words.

1.How did Franz's feelings about M. Hamel change on the day of the last lesson?

Show answer

Franz had feared M. Hamel's ruler and found him strict. Seeing him in his Sunday clothes, speaking gently and sadly, Franz felt sorry and forgot the scoldings. He understood that the teacher had served faithfully for forty years, and he respected and pitied him.

2.Why were the village elders sitting in the classroom on the last day?

Show answer

They came to thank M. Hamel for forty years of faithful service, to show respect for the country that was no longer theirs, and out of regret for not having learnt French when they had the chance.

3.Why does Anees Jung call Saheb's new job a loss of freedom?

Show answer

At the tea stall Saheb earns ₹800 and meals, but he is no longer his own master. The steel canister is heavier than his plastic bag; the carefree ragpicker has become a servant to his employer.

4.What is the "vicious circle" in Lost Spring?

Show answer

The bangle makers are trapped by the sahukars, middlemen, policemen, keepers of law, bureaucrats and politicians. Poverty and tradition crush initiative, so they cannot organise or escape.

5.How did Douglas finally overcome his fear of water?

Show answer

He hired an instructor who taught him step by step for seven months. To remove the remaining terror he swam alone in Lake Wentworth, Warm Lake and the Tieton River until he knew the fear had gone.

6.What made the peddler change in The Rattrap?

Show answer

Edla Willmansson treated him with kindness and respect even after learning he was a thief. Her trust awakened his goodness, so he returned the stolen money and signed as Captain von Stahle.

7.Why did Gandhi agree to a 25% refund in Champaran?

Show answer

The amount mattered less than the principle. The refund forced the landlords to give up part of their prestige and admit wrongdoing, which broke their hold and gave the peasants courage.

8.How did Gandhi teach self-reliance at Champaran?

Show answer

He refused C.F. Andrews' help, saying the peasants must fight their own battle without leaning on an Englishman. He also worked on their health, sanitation and education.

9.Why did Subbu seem indispensable at Gemini Studios?

Show answer

He gave filmmaking a direction, offered many ideas when the boss was stuck, was a poet, novelist and fine actor, and was loyal and generous — though his closeness to the boss made others resent him.

10.Why was the English poet's visit an "unexplained mystery"?

Show answer

Stephen Spender spoke in an English accent on poetry to Tamil film workers, who did not understand him; no one knew why he had been invited.

11.What are "interstices" for Umberto Eco?

Show answer

Empty spaces in time — moments like waiting for a lift — that he uses to write, which explains how he produces so much.

12.Contrast Sophie and Jansie.

Show answer

Sophie is a dreamer who imagines boutiques and fame and fantasises about Danny Casey. Jansie is practical, knows they will work in the biscuit factory, and warns Sophie not to build castles in the air.

13.What does the poet see outside the car in My Mother at Sixty-six, and why?

Show answer

Young trees sprinting and merry children spilling out of their homes — images of life and youth that contrast with her ageing, corpse-like mother.

14.What does Neruda want us to achieve by keeping quiet?

Show answer

A moment of introspection when all activity stops — no wars, no harm to nature — so that people understand themselves and one another and feel togetherness.

15.According to Keats, what are some things of beauty?

Show answer

The sun, the moon, old and young trees, daffodils, clear rills, the musk-rose, the grandeur of the mighty dead and lovely tales.

16.Whom does Frost call "greedy good-doers"?

Show answer

City people and planners who promise to help the rural poor but actually exploit them and relocate them for their own gain.

17.What did the Third Level represent for Charley?

Show answer

An escape from the stress, insecurity and war of modern life into the peaceful world of Galesburg in 1894.

18.How was the Tiger King's death ironic?

Show answer

Having killed ninety-nine tigers to defeat the prophecy, he died from a splinter of a cheap wooden tiger bought for his son.

19.Why is Antarctica the right place to study Earth's past and future?

Show answer

It holds 650 million years of history in its ice and is a simple, pristine ecosystem where small changes, like global warming, show their effects quickly.

20.How did Zitkala-Sa resist the cutting of her hair?

Show answer

She hid under a bed and struggled when dragged out; for her, shingled hair was worn by cowards and mourners, so it meant losing her identity.

4-mark writing tasks

Notice or formal invitation/reply in about 50 words. Answers give the content points the examiner looks for.

1.Write a notice for your school notice board about an inter-school science quiz.

Show answer

School name, NOTICE, date, heading. Content: organiser, date, time, venue, eligibility (team of two, Classes XI–XII), last date and person to register with, prizes. Name and designation.

2.As Secretary of the Eco Club, write a notice about a tree-plantation drive.

Show answer

Event, date and time, place, what to bring (gloves, saplings provided), who can volunteer, contact person, deadline to give names.

3.Write a notice informing students about a blood-donation camp organised by your school.

Show answer

Date, venue, time, organisers (e.g. Red Cross), eligibility (18+, weight above 45 kg, healthy), benefits, registration details.

4.Write a notice about a lost wristwatch in the school library.

Show answer

Description (brand, colour, strap), when and where lost, request to return to the office/undersigned, contact details, reward if any.

5.Write a notice announcing a five-day yoga workshop for students.

Show answer

Dates, time, venue, instructor's name, benefits, dress code, how to register, last date.

6.As Head Girl, write a notice inviting entries for the school magazine.

Show answer

Types of entries (articles, poems, art), word limits, last date, where to submit, theme if any.

7.Write a notice for your residential society about a water-supply disruption.

Show answer

Society name, date, reason (repair of overhead tank), date and time of disruption, request to store water, apology, Secretary's name.

8.Write a notice about a career-guidance seminar for Class XII.

Show answer

Topic, resource person, date, time, venue, who should attend, registration, what to bring (questions).

9.Write a notice about the school's annual sports day.

Show answer

Date, events, last date to register with the sports teacher, house-wise participation, dress, rehearsals.

10.Write a notice announcing a visit to a science museum.

Show answer

Date, departure and return time, cost, consent form deadline, items to carry, teacher in charge.

11.Draft a formal invitation to the inauguration of your school's science exhibition.

Show answer

Third person ("The Principal, staff and students … request the pleasure of your company …"), occasion, chief guest, date, time, venue, RSVP. No signature.

12.Draft an invitation for your school's annual day celebration.

Show answer

Third person; occasion; chief guest; date, day, time; venue; programme highlights; RSVP with name/number.

13.Draft an invitation to a book fair organised by your school.

Show answer

Event, dates, timings, venue, special attractions (author talks), RSVP or "All are welcome".

14.You are Dr Anjali Rao. Accept an invitation to be chief guest at a science exhibition.

Show answer

Formal letter: address, date, receiver, subject. Thank for the invitation (refer to event/date), accept, confirm arrival time, good wishes. Yours faithfully.

15.Decline an invitation to judge an inter-school debate.

Show answer

Thank, express regret, give a reason (prior commitment out of town), wish success, offer help another time.

16.Accept an invitation from your friend's parents to her wedding (formal reply).

Show answer

Third person or formal letter; thank; confirm attendance; congratulate.

17.Decline an invitation to a school's silver-jubilee function as a former teacher.

Show answer

Thank; regret; reason (health); good wishes for the jubilee.

18.Draft an invitation to parents for a parent–teacher meeting.

Show answer

Third-person or formal: purpose, date, time, venue, request to bring report card, RSVP.

19.Draft an invitation for the inauguration of a new school library.

Show answer

Occasion, chief guest, date, time, venue, RSVP. Third person, no signature.

20.Accept an invitation to deliver a talk on art therapy at a school.

Show answer

Thank, accept, confirm topic/duration, mention requirements (projector), confirm time, sign off.

5-mark questions

Writing in 120–150 words and long literature answers. Value points are given.

1.Write a letter to the editor about the growing problem of stray dogs in your locality.

Show answer

Format (addresses, date, subject, salutation). Points: incidents/bites, fear among children and elderly, rabies risk, garbage attracting dogs; suggestions — sterilisation drives, shelters, waste management, awareness. Close: hope for action.

2.Write a letter to the editor on water-logging after rain.

Show answer

Problem, causes (blocked drains, encroachments), effects (traffic, disease, damage), suggestions (desilting, proper drainage, accountability).

3.Write a letter to the editor about rising e-waste.

Show answer

Sources, hazards (lead, mercury), informal recycling dangers; suggestions — collection centres, buy-back, awareness, laws.

4.Write a letter to the editor about noise pollution from loudspeakers.

Show answer

Health effects, students' studies, elderly; suggestions — time limits, enforcement, decibel limits.

5.Write a letter to the editor highlighting the need for more public libraries.

Show answer

Value of reading, decline due to screens, cost of books; suggestions — community libraries, digital access, reading clubs.

6.Apply for the post of Data Analyst advertised by a company, with a bio-data.

Show answer

Letter: reference to the advertisement, interest, brief qualifications, request for interview, enclosure. Bio-data: personal details, qualifications table, experience, skills (Python, SQL), languages, references, declaration.

7.Apply for the post of a school counsellor, with a résumé.

Show answer

Advertisement reference; qualifications (psychology, counselling); experience with adolescents; skills; references.

8.Write an article "Reclaiming Attention in the Digital Age".

Show answer

Title, byline; hook; causes (notifications, reels); effects (attention, sleep, grades); solutions (screen-free hours, reading, apps, role models); conclusion.

9.Write an article on "Artificial Intelligence in Education: Opportunity or Challenge?"

Show answer

Benefits (personalised learning, access), challenges (cheating, over-dependence, privacy, digital divide), balanced use and teacher's role, conclusion.

10.Write an article on the importance of mental health for students.

Show answer

Exam stress, social media pressure; signs; remedies (talking, exercise, sleep, counsellors); role of schools and parents.

11.Write an article on the need to save water.

Show answer

Facts on scarcity; causes; effects; measures (rainwater harvesting, fixing leaks, reuse); call to action.

12.Write a report for your school magazine on a talk by ISRO scientists.

Show answer

Heading, byline, place/date. 5 Ws; chief guest; highlights (talk, models, Q&A); students' response; vote of thanks. Past tense.

13.Write a newspaper report on a health check-up camp in your school.

Show answer

Heading, byline, place and date; organiser; doctors; tests done; number of beneficiaries; advice given; impact.

14.Write a report on Independence Day celebrations in your school.

Show answer

Flag hoisting, chief guest, cultural programme, speeches, prizes, sweets, conclusion.

15.Write a report on a tree-plantation drive.

Show answer

Organiser, date, venue, number of saplings, participants, guest, message, follow-up care.

16.How does Gandhi's work in Champaran show his method of satyagraha and his concern for the poor? (Indigo)

Show answer

Investigation of facts; civil disobedience when ordered to leave; peaceful mass support; 25% refund as a moral victory; self-reliance; social work in health and education; freedom from fear.

17."The world is a rattrap." Discuss how this idea is proved false by the end of The Rattrap.

Show answer

Peddler's cynicism; his own fall into the trap by stealing; Edla's unconditional kindness; his awakening; returning the money; signing as captain — essential goodness can redeem anyone.

18.How does Dr Sadao reconcile his duty as a doctor with his duty to his nation? (The Enemy)

Show answer

Saves the soldier (medical ethics); informs the General (loyalty); General's selfishness; helps the soldier escape; betrays neither; Hana's compassion — humanity above enmity.

19.Compare how Zitkala-Sa and Bama resist discrimination. (Memories of Childhood)

Show answer

Racial vs caste discrimination; Zitkala-Sa's open physical resistance and grief at losing identity; Bama's realisation through the vadai incident and resolve to excel in studies; both show self-respect and awareness.

20.How does Mr Lamb help Derry to change? (On the Face of It)

Show answer

Open gate and garden as symbols; ignores his own tin leg; treats Derry as normal; teaches that inner worth matters; Derry decides to go back and face the world — a tragic but hopeful ending.
