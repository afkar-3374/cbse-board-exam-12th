1-mark questions

Q1–16 are MCQs; Q17–20 are Assertion–Reason. A–R options: (a) both true, R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) both false.

1.A closed surface encloses charges +q and −q. The net electric flux through it is

  1. q/ε₀
  2. 2q/ε₀
  3. zero
  4. −q/ε₀
Show answer

(c) zero — the net enclosed charge is zero.

2.The electric field of a short dipole at a distance r varies as

  1. 1/r
  2. 1/r²
  3. 1/r³
  4. 1/r⁴
Show answer

(c) 1/r³

3.A parallel plate capacitor C has its plate separation halved and is completely filled with a dielectric of constant K. The new capacitance is

  1. KC/2
  2. KC
  3. 2KC
  4. 4KC
Show answer

(c) 2KC

4.At a fixed potential difference, the length of a wire is doubled. The drift velocity of electrons becomes

  1. double
  2. half
  3. same
  4. four times
Show answer

(b) half — vd = eVτ/(mL).

5.A charged particle moves parallel to a uniform magnetic field. The magnetic force on it is

  1. qvB
  2. qvB/2
  3. zero
  4. maximum
Show answer

(c) zero — sin 0° = 0.

6.The susceptibility of a paramagnetic sample at 300 K is χ. At 600 K it will be

  1. 2χ
  2. χ
  3. χ/2
  4. χ/4
Show answer

(c) χ/2 — Curie's law χ ∝ 1/T.

7.The number of turns of a solenoid is doubled (same length and area). Its self-inductance becomes

  1. L/2
  2. L
  3. 2L
  4. 4L
Show answer

(d) 4L — L ∝ N².

8.The power factor of a series LCR circuit at resonance is

  1. 0
  2. 0.5
  3. 1/√2
  4. 1
Show answer

(d) 1 — the circuit is purely resistive.

9.The electromagnetic waves used in LASIK eye surgery are

  1. infrared
  2. ultraviolet
  3. microwaves
  4. X-rays
Show answer

(b) ultraviolet

10.The critical angle for a medium of refractive index √2 is

  1. 30°
  2. 45°
  3. 60°
  4. 90°
Show answer

(b) 45° — sin C = 1/√2.

11.A YDSE apparatus is immersed in water (n = 4/3). The fringe width becomes

  1. 4β/3
  2. 3β/4
  3. β
  4. 16β/9
Show answer

(b) 3β/4

12.In the photoelectric effect, the stopping potential depends on

  1. intensity of light
  2. frequency of light
  3. area of the plate
  4. time of exposure
Show answer

(b) frequency of light (and the metal's work function).

13.The de Broglie wavelength of an electron accelerated through 100 V is about

  1. 0.0123 nm
  2. 0.123 nm
  3. 1.23 nm
  4. 12.3 nm
Show answer

(b) 0.123 nm — λ = 1.227/√V nm.

14.The energy needed to ionise a hydrogen atom from its first excited state is

  1. 13.6 eV
  2. 10.2 eV
  3. 3.4 eV
  4. 1.51 eV
Show answer

(c) 3.4 eV

15.Nuclear density, as the mass number A increases,

  1. increases
  2. decreases
  3. remains nearly constant
  4. first increases then decreases
Show answer

(c) remains nearly constant (R ∝ A1/3).

16.The majority charge carriers in a p-type semiconductor are

  1. electrons
  2. holes
  3. positive ions
  4. negative ions
Show answer

(b) holes

17.A: Two equipotential surfaces never intersect. R: At the point of intersection there would be two values of potential, which is impossible.

Show answer

(a) Both true; R explains A.

18.A: Increasing the intensity of incident light increases the stopping potential. R: Stopping potential depends on the number of photons striking the surface.

Show answer

(d) Both false — intensity changes only the photocurrent; stopping potential depends on frequency.

19.A: Lenz's law is a consequence of the law of conservation of energy. R: The induced current always opposes the change that produces it.

Show answer

(a) Both true; R explains A (work must be done against the opposition, which becomes electrical energy).

20.A: A bulb glows almost instantly when switched on, although the drift speed of electrons is only mm/s. R: The free-electron density in metals is very high.

Show answer

(b) Both true, but R is not the explanation — the electric field is set up throughout the wire almost instantly, so all electrons start drifting together.

2-mark questions

Very short answer (about 30–40 words or a short calculation).

1.Charges +2 μC and +8 μC are 30 cm apart. Find the electric potential at the midpoint.

Show answer

V = k(q₁ + q₂)/r = 9×10⁹ × 10×10⁻⁶ / 0.15 = 6 × 10⁵ V.

2.A dipole of moment 4 × 10⁻⁹ C m is at 30° to a field of 5 × 10⁴ N/C. Find the torque and the work needed to turn it from 0° to 180°.

Show answer

τ = pE sin 30° = 4×10⁻⁹ × 5×10⁴ × ½ = 10⁻⁴ N m. W = 2pE = 4 × 10⁻⁴ J.

3.A 10 μF capacitor charged to 100 V is disconnected and a dielectric (K = 5) fills it. Find the new C, V and energy.

Show answer

C = 50 μF; Q = 10⁻³ C stays, so V = 20 V. U: ½ × 10⁻⁵ × 10⁴ = 0.05 J → ½ × 5×10⁻⁵ × 400 = 0.01 J (energy falls to U/K).

4.A copper wire (A = 1.5 × 10⁻⁶ m², n = 8.5 × 10²⁸ m⁻³) carries 3 A. Find the drift velocity.

Show answer

vd = I/(neA) = 3/(8.5×10²⁸ × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 1.5×10⁻⁶) ≈ 1.47 × 10⁻⁴ m/s.

5.A cell (E = 6 V, r = 1 Ω) is connected to 5 Ω. Find the current and terminal voltage. What would the terminal voltage be while charging it at 1 A?

Show answer

I = 6/6 = 1 A; V = E − Ir = 5 V. While charging: V = E + Ir = 7 V.

6.A wire is stretched to three times its length. How do its resistance and resistivity change?

Show answer

Volume constant ⇒ A becomes A/3; R = ρL/A becomes 9R. Resistivity is unchanged (depends only on material and temperature).

7.A galvanometer (G = 50 Ω, Ig = 2 mA) is to be converted into a 0–10 V voltmeter. Find the series resistance.

Show answer

R = V/Ig − G = 10/0.002 − 50 = 4950 Ω in series.

8.Two long parallel wires 10 cm apart carry 2 A and 3 A in the same direction. Find the force per metre and its nature.

Show answer

F/L = μ₀I₁I₂/2πd = 2×10⁻⁷ × 6/0.1 = 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ N/m, attractive.

9.Give two differences between diamagnetic and paramagnetic substances.

Show answer

Diamagnetic: χ small negative, weakly repelled, moves to weaker field, independent of temperature. Paramagnetic: χ small positive, weakly attracted, moves to stronger field, χ ∝ 1/T.

10.A coil of 100 turns and area 0.02 m² is in a field that changes from 0.2 T to 0.8 T in 0.5 s. Find the induced emf.

Show answer

ε = NAΔB/Δt = 100 × 0.02 × 0.6/0.5 = 2.4 V.

11.Why is the current in a pure inductor called wattless?

Show answer

Current lags voltage by π/2, so power factor cos φ = 0 and average power P = VrmsIrms cos φ = 0. Energy stored in one quarter-cycle is returned in the next.

12.A step-down transformer converts 220 V to 11 V; primary has 2000 turns. Find secondary turns and primary current if the secondary current is 4 A (ideal).

Show answer

Ns = 2000 × 11/220 = 100. Ip = IsVs/Vp = 4 × 11/220 = 0.2 A.

13.An EM wave has frequency 6 × 10¹⁴ Hz and E₀ = 60 V/m. Find λ, its region and B₀.

Show answer

λ = c/f = 5 × 10⁻⁷ m = 500 nm (visible). B₀ = E₀/c = 2 × 10⁻⁷ T.

14.What is displacement current? Find it for a 20 μF capacitor whose voltage changes at 3 V/s.

Show answer

Id = ε₀ dΦE/dt, current due to a changing electric field. Id = C dV/dt = 20×10⁻⁶ × 3 = 60 μA (equal to the conduction current).

15.Lenses of +5 D and −2 D are in contact. Find the focal length and nature.

Show answer

P = +3 D ⇒ f = 100/3 ≈ 33.3 cm, converging.

16.Find the critical angle of glass (n = 1.5). State the two conditions for TIR.

Show answer

sin C = 1/1.5 = 2/3 ⇒ C ≈ 42°. Conditions: light goes from denser to rarer medium; angle of incidence > C.

17.In YDSE, λ = 600 nm, D = 1.2 m, d = 0.3 mm. Find the fringe width.

Show answer

β = λD/d = 600×10⁻⁹ × 1.2/0.3×10⁻³ = 2.4 mm.

18.Light of 400 nm falls on a metal of work function 2.2 eV. Find Kmax and stopping potential.

Show answer

E = 1240/400 = 3.1 eV ⇒ Kmax = 0.9 eV, V₀ = 0.9 V.

19.Calculate the wavelength emitted when an electron in hydrogen jumps from n = 3 to n = 2 (R = 1.1 × 10⁷ m⁻¹). Name the series.

Show answer

1/λ = R(1/4 − 1/9) = 5R/36 ⇒ λ = 36/(5 × 1.1×10⁷) ≈ 655 nm, Balmer (visible, red).

20.The mass defect of ⁴He is 0.0304 u. Find its binding energy per nucleon.

Show answer

BE = 0.0304 × 931.5 ≈ 28.3 MeV; BE/A ≈ 7.07 MeV.

3-mark questions

Short answer — derivations or two-step numericals.

1.Using Gauss's law, derive the field due to an infinite plane sheet of charge density σ.

Show answer

Pill-box Gaussian surface through the sheet; flux only through the two flat faces: 2EA = σA/ε₀ ⇒ E = σ/2ε₀, independent of distance, directed away from a positive sheet. Notes

2.Two cells (2 V, 1 Ω) and (4 V, 2 Ω) are connected in parallel. Find the equivalent emf and internal resistance.

Show answer

E = (E₁r₂ + E₂r₁)/(r₁ + r₂) = (2·2 + 4·1)/3 = 8/3 V; r = r₁r₂/(r₁ + r₂) = 2/3 Ω.

3.Using Kirchhoff's rules, obtain the balance condition of a Wheatstone bridge.

Show answer

Apply the loop rule to ABDA and BCDB with Ig = 0: I₁P = I₂R and I₁Q = I₂S ⇒ P/Q = R/S. Notes

4.Write B on the axis of a circular loop. Find the ratio of the field at x = R to that at the centre.

Show answer

B = μ₀IR²/2(R² + x²)3/2. At x = R: μ₀I/(2R · 2√2). Ratio = 1 : 2√2.

5.Using Ampere's law, find B inside a long solenoid. Calculate it for 1000 turns/m carrying 5 A.

Show answer

Rectangular loop: B·L = μ₀nLI ⇒ B = μ₀nI = 4π×10⁻⁷ × 1000 × 5 ≈ 6.28 × 10⁻³ T.

6.A rod of 0.5 m moves at 4 m/s on rails ⟂ to B = 0.3 T; circuit resistance 0.6 Ω. Find emf, current and the force needed to keep it moving.

Show answer

ε = Blv = 0.6 V; I = 1 A; F = IlB = 0.15 N (power 0.6 W = I²R).

7.A series LCR circuit has R = 30 Ω, XL = 70 Ω, XC = 30 Ω across 200 V. Find Z, I and the phase angle.

Show answer

Z = √(30² + 40²) = 50 Ω; I = 4 A; tan φ = 40/30 ⇒ φ ≈ 53°, current lags.

8.An AC generator has 200 turns of area 0.02 m², rotating at 50 rad/s in 0.5 T. Find the peak and rms emf.

Show answer

ε₀ = NBAω = 200 × 0.5 × 0.02 × 50 = 100 V; εrms = 100/√2 ≈ 70.7 V.

9.A convex lens (n = 1.5) has f = 20 cm in air. Find its focal length in water (n = 4/3).

Show answer

fw/fa = (n − 1)/(n/nw − 1) = 0.5/(1.125 − 1) = 4 ⇒ 80 cm.

10.A prism of angle 60° gives minimum deviation 30°. Find n and the angle of incidence at minimum deviation.

Show answer

n = sin 45°/sin 30° = √2; i = (A + δm)/2 = 45°.

11.A microscope has f₀ = 1 cm, fₑ = 5 cm, tube length 20 cm. Find M for final image at infinity and at the near point.

Show answer

Infinity: (L/f₀)(D/fₑ) = 20 × 5 = 100. Near point: 20 × (1 + 25/5) = 120.

12.Using Huygens' principle, derive Snell's law.

Show answer

Wavefront AB, wavelets: BC = v₁t, AE = v₂t. sin i = v₁t/AC, sin r = v₂t/AC ⇒ sin i/sin r = v₁/v₂ = n₂/n₁. Notes

13.Light of 600 nm passes a 0.2 mm slit; screen at 1.5 m. Find the width of the central maximum. Why do secondary maxima get weaker?

Show answer

Width = 2λD/a = 2 × 600×10⁻⁹ × 1.5/0.2×10⁻³ = 9 mm. Higher orders get contributions from a smaller part of the slit, so they are dimmer.

14.Two wavelengths 650 nm and 520 nm are used in YDSE (d = 2 mm, D = 1.2 m). Find the least distance where bright fringes coincide.

Show answer

4 × 650 = 5 × 520 = 2600 nm ⇒ y = 2600×10⁻⁹ × 1.2/2×10⁻³ = 1.56 mm.

15.Draw photocurrent vs anode potential for (i) different intensities, same ν; (ii) different ν, same intensity. Explain.

Show answer

(i) same stopping potential, saturation current ∝ intensity; (ii) same saturation current, higher ν gives a more negative stopping potential. Notes

16.An electron and a proton have the same kinetic energy. Which has the longer de Broglie wavelength? Find the ratio.

Show answer

λ = h/√(2mK) ⇒ λe/λp = √(mp/me) ≈ √1836 ≈ 43; the electron's λ is longer.

17.Using Bohr's postulates, show that rₙ ∝ n².

Show answer

mv²/r = ke²/r² and mvr = nh/2π ⇒ rₙ = ε₀n²h²/(πme²) = 0.53 n² Å.

18.Draw the BE/A curve and use it to explain energy release in fission and fusion.

Show answer

Peak ≈ 8.8 MeV at A ≈ 56; heavy nuclei split into medium nuclei of higher BE/A (fission), light nuclei combine into nuclei of higher BE/A (fusion); increase in total BE is released. Notes

19.Derive the distance of closest approach of an α-particle of kinetic energy K to a nucleus of charge Ze. What happens if K is doubled?

Show answer

K = (1/4πε₀)(2e)(Ze)/d ⇒ d = 2Ze²/(4πε₀K). Doubling K halves d.

20.With a diagram, explain a full-wave rectifier.

Show answer

Centre-tap transformer + two diodes; each conducts in alternate half-cycles and current through RL is always in the same direction; output frequency 2f. Notes

4-mark case-based questions

Read the situation and answer the parts (1 + 1 + 2 or 1 + 1 + 1 + 1).

1.A camera flash uses a 500 μF capacitor charged to 300 V, discharged in 1 ms. (i) Energy stored? (ii) Average power to the lamp? (iii) Effect of doubling C at the same voltage? (iv) Why use a capacitor rather than the battery directly?

Show answer

(i) ½CV² = 22.5 J (ii) 22.5 J/10⁻³ s = 22.5 kW (iii) energy doubles (iv) a capacitor can release its stored energy almost instantly, giving a huge power a battery cannot supply.

2.A 6 μF capacitor is charged by a 10 V battery, disconnected, then filled with a slab of K = 3. Find (i) initial charge (ii) new capacitance (iii) new voltage (iv) new energy.

Show answer

(i) 60 μC (ii) 18 μF (iii) 10/3 V (iv) Q²/2C = (60×10⁻⁶)²/(2 × 18×10⁻⁶) = 100 μJ (it was 300 μJ).

3.During a thunderstorm it is safest to stay inside a car. (i) What is the field inside a closed conductor? (ii) Where does charge reside? (iii) Direction of the field just outside a conductor? (iv) Explain the safety.

Show answer

(i) Zero (ii) on the outer surface (iii) normal to the surface, E = σ/ε₀ (iv) the metal body acts as an electrostatic shield; charge flows over the outside to the ground.

4.A galvanometer has G = 20 Ω and full-scale current 2 mA. (i) Why a soft-iron core? (ii) Why a radial field? (iii) Shunt for 0–6 A? (iv) Series resistance for 0–20 V?

Show answer

(i) increases B and sensitivity (ii) torque ∝ I, linear scale (iii) S = 0.002 × 20/5.998 ≈ 6.7 × 10⁻³ Ω (iv) R = 20/0.002 − 20 = 9980 Ω.

5.In a velocity selector E = 2 × 10⁴ V/m and B = 0.1 T. (i) Speed of undeflected ions? (ii) Why does magnetic force do no work? (iii) Ratio of radii of a proton and α-particle with equal KE in the same B? (iv) Does the period depend on speed?

Show answer

(i) v = E/B = 2 × 10⁵ m/s (ii) force ⟂ velocity (iii) r = √(2mK)/qB ⇒ 1 : 1 (iv) no, T = 2πm/qB.

6.Eddy-current brakes in trains. (i) Laws behind them? (ii) Flux through a region changes from 0.5 Wb to 0.2 Wb in 0.1 s; emf? (iii) Where does the KE go? (iv) How are eddy losses reduced in transformers?

Show answer

(i) Faraday's and Lenz's laws (ii) 3 V (iii) heat (I²R) in the disc (iv) laminated cores.

7.A lab transformer has 1150 primary and 230 secondary turns on 200 V. (i) Secondary voltage? (ii) Primary current when the secondary gives 2 A? (iii) Output power at 75% efficiency (same input)? (iv) Will it work on DC?

Show answer

(i) 40 V (ii) 0.4 A (iii) input 80 W ⇒ output 60 W (iv) no — steady DC gives no changing flux, so no induced emf.

8.A radio is tuned with a series LCR circuit. (i) Condition for resonance? (ii) f₀ formula? (iii) Effect of R on sharpness (Q-factor)? (iv) ω₀ for L = 2 H, C = 32 μF?

Show answer

(i) XL = XC (ii) f₀ = 1/(2π√LC) (iii) smaller R ⇒ higher Q, sharper peak, better selectivity (iv) 1/√(64×10⁻⁶) = 125 rad/s.

9.An optical fibre has core n = 1.5 and cladding n = 1.2. (i) Condition for TIR? (ii) Critical angle? (iii) Will a ray at 60° be totally reflected (sin 60° = 0.87)? (iv) Why must the cladding have lower n?

Show answer

(i) denser → rarer, i > C (ii) sin C = 1.2/1.5 = 0.8 (iii) yes, 0.87 > 0.8 (iv) TIR is possible only from a denser to a rarer medium.

10.Object and screen are fixed 80 cm apart; a convex lens gives sharp images at two positions 20 cm apart. (i) f? (ii) Condition for two positions? (iii) Nature of the two images? (iv) Can f = 25 cm work here?

Show answer

(i) f = (D² − d²)/4D = 18.75 cm (ii) D ≥ 4f (iii) one enlarged, one diminished (iv) no, 4f = 100 cm > 80 cm.

11.A wireless phone charger uses mutual induction. (i) What happens to the induced emf if the coils move apart? (ii) Why a soft ferromagnetic core? (iii) Why does the primary feel more opposition when the phone draws power? (iv) Why must the source be AC?

Show answer

(i) decreases (less flux linkage) (ii) high permeability, low hysteresis loss (iii) secondary current produces opposing flux (Lenz), increasing primary's back-emf effect (iv) only changing current gives changing flux.

12.A YDSE set-up in water is moved into a liquid of higher n. (i) Effect on speed, frequency, wavelength? (ii) On fringe width? (iii) On the central maximum? (iv) Shape of fringes?

Show answer

(i) speed and λ decrease, frequency same (ii) decreases (β/n) (iii) stays at the centre (iv) remain straight.

13.Single-slit diffraction with red light. (i) Effect of replacing red with blue? (ii) Of narrowing the slit? (iii) Why is the central maximum twice as wide? (iv) Condition for noticeable diffraction?

Show answer

(i) bands narrower, closer (ii) central maximum wider and dimmer (iii) it spans from −λ/a to +λ/a while others span λ/a (iv) slit width comparable to λ.

14.A V₀–ν graph for a metal is a straight line cutting the ν-axis at 5 × 10¹⁴ Hz. (i) What does the slope give? (ii) What is the intercept? (iii) Kmax for 8 × 10¹⁴ Hz? (iv) Effect of doubling intensity on V₀?

Show answer

(i) h/e (ii) threshold frequency (iii) h(3×10¹⁴) = 1.24 eV (iv) none.

15.An electron microscope accelerates electrons through V. (i) How does λ vary with V? (ii) Effect of raising V from 100 V to 400 V? (iii) Momentum for λ = 0.1 nm? (iv) Why does it resolve finer detail than an optical microscope?

Show answer

(i) λ ∝ 1/√V (ii) λ halves (iii) p = h/λ = 6.63 × 10⁻²⁴ kg m/s (iv) electron λ ≪ visible λ.

16.A hydrogen electron has total energy −3.4 eV. (i) n? (ii) KE and PE? (iii) Wavelength emitted in a jump from n = 3 to this level? (iv) Number of lines from n = 4?

Show answer

(i) n = 2 (ii) KE 3.4 eV, PE −6.8 eV (iii) 656 nm, Balmer (iv) n(n − 1)/2 = 6.

17.Nuclei W (A ≈ 240), X (A ≈ 120), Y (A ≈ 56), Z (A ≈ 4) lie on the BE/A curve. (i) Which is most stable? (ii) Which undergoes fission? (iii) Which fusion? (iv) Why is BE/A nearly constant for 30 < A < 170?

Show answer

(i) Y (ii) W (iii) Z (iv) nuclear force is short-range and saturates.

18.Nuclear force. (i) Two properties? (ii) Is it charge dependent? (iii) Nature below 0.8 fm? (iv) Why is nuclear density constant?

Show answer

(i) strongest, short-range (a few fm), saturating (ii) no — p–p, n–n, n–p nearly equal (iii) strongly repulsive (iv) R ∝ A1/3 so volume ∝ A.

19.A 50 Hz supply feeds a rectifier. (i) Output frequency of a half-wave and full-wave rectifier? (ii) Which diode conducts in each half? (iii) Role of a capacitor filter? (iv) Why is the full-wave more efficient?

Show answer

(i) 50 Hz, 100 Hz (ii) D₁ in one half, D₂ in the other (iii) smooths ripple (iv) both halves of the input are used.

20.Silicon (ni = 1.5 × 10¹⁶ m⁻³) is doped with 4.5 × 10²² m⁻³ arsenic atoms. (i) Type? (ii) Majority carriers? (iii) Hole concentration? (iv) Is the crystal charged?

Show answer

(i) n-type (ii) electrons (iii) nh = ni²/ne = 5 × 10⁹ m⁻³ (iv) no, electrically neutral.

5-mark questions

Long answer — derivation (3) + numerical (2). Answers give the key points; full working is in the notes.

1.Derive E on the equatorial line of a dipole. Find E at 20 cm on the equatorial line of p = 4 × 10⁻⁹ C m.

Show answer

E = (1/4πε₀)p/(r² + a²)3/2 ≈ p/(4πε₀r³), opposite to p. Numerical: 9×10⁹ × 4×10⁻⁹/0.008 = 4500 N/C. Notes

2.Derive C for a parallel plate capacitor with a dielectric slab of thickness t. Find C for A = 200 cm², d = 2 mm, t = 1 mm, K = 5.

Show answer

C = ε₀A/(d − t + t/K). Numerical: 8.854×10⁻¹² × 0.02/(1.2 × 10⁻³) ≈ 148 pF.

3.Using Gauss's law, find E inside and outside a charged spherical shell; plot E vs r. Find V at the centre of a shell of radius 0.2 m carrying 6 μC.

Show answer

Outside q/(4πε₀r²), inside 0. V(centre) = V(surface) = 9×10⁹ × 6×10⁻⁶/0.2 = 2.7 × 10⁵ V.

4.Derive the energy stored in a capacitor. 2, 3, 6 μF are joined in series then parallel across 12 V; find the ratio of energies.

Show answer

U = Q²/2C = ½CV². Series C = 1 μF ⇒ 72 μJ; parallel 11 μF ⇒ 792 μJ; ratio 11 : 1.

5.Derive I = neAvd and ρ = m/ne²τ. Explain how ρ of a metal varies with temperature.

Show answer

vd = eEτ/m; charge crossing area: I = neAvd ⇒ j = (ne²τ/m)E. In metals τ falls with rising T ⇒ ρ rises. Notes

6.Using Biot–Savart, derive B on the axis of a circular loop. Two identical coaxial coils carry equal currents in the same sense — do their fields add at the midpoint?

Show answer

B = μ₀NIR²/2(R² + x²)3/2. Same sense ⇒ fields point the same way along the axis and add.

7.Derive the torque on a rectangular coil in a uniform field. Find the torque on a coil of 100 turns, area 0.05 m², current 2 A, with its plane parallel to 0.4 T.

Show answer

τ = NIAB sin θ (θ between normal and B). Plane ∥ B ⇒ θ = 90°: τ = 100 × 2 × 0.05 × 0.4 = 4 N m.

8.Derive the force per unit length between parallel currents and define the ampere. Find it for 10 A and 15 A, 5 cm apart.

Show answer

F/L = μ₀I₁I₂/2πd. Numerical: 2×10⁻⁷ × 150/0.05 = 6 × 10⁻⁴ N/m, attractive. 1 A gives 2 × 10⁻⁷ N/m between wires 1 m apart.

9.Derive the self-inductance of a long solenoid. Find L for N = 500, l = 0.5 m, A = 2 × 10⁻⁴ m², and the energy stored at 5 A.

Show answer

L = μ₀N²A/l ≈ 1.26 × 10⁻⁴ H; U = ½LI² ≈ 1.57 × 10⁻³ J.

10.Explain the AC generator and derive ε = NBAω sin ωt. Find ε₀ for 100 turns, 0.1 m², 60 rev/s, 0.2 T.

Show answer

Flux NBA cos ωt ⇒ ε = NBAω sin ωt; ω = 120π. ε₀ = 100 × 0.2 × 0.1 × 120π ≈ 754 V.

11.Using a phasor diagram, derive Z and φ for series LCR. For R = 10 Ω, L = 0.1 H, C = 10 μF on 100 V, find ω₀ and the current at resonance.

Show answer

Z = √(R² + (XL − XC)²), tan φ = (XL − XC)/R. ω₀ = 1/√(10⁻⁶) = 1000 rad/s; I = 100/10 = 10 A.
