1. Energy bands

Q. Distinguish conductors, semiconductors and insulators on the basis of energy bands.3 M

  • Conductor: valence and conduction bands overlap (or CB partly filled) → many free electrons.
  • Semiconductor: small gap (< 3 eV). At 0 K it is an insulator; at room temperature some electrons jump to CB.
  • Insulator: large gap, so thermal energy cannot lift electrons to CB.

2. Intrinsic & extrinsic semiconductors

Q. What is an intrinsic semiconductor? Explain n-type and p-type doping with diagrams.3 M

Intrinsic: pure Si/Ge. Each electron that breaks a bond leaves a hole: ne = nh = ni.

n-typep-type
Dopantpentavalent (P, As, Sb) — donortrivalent (B, Al, In, Ga) — acceptor
Majority carrierselectronsholes
Energy leveldonor level just below CB (~0.01 eV)acceptor level just above VB
Relationne ≫ nhnh ≫ ne
ne · nh = ni²

Both n- and p-type crystals are electrically neutral — the dopant atom is neutral; only the carrier balance changes.

Example: ni = 1.5×10¹⁶ m⁻³, doped with 4.5×10²² m⁻³ pentavalent atoms → ne ≈ 4.5×10²², nh = ni²/ne = 5×10⁹ m⁻³.

3. p–n junction formation

Q. Explain the formation of the depletion layer and potential barrier in a p–n junction.3 M

  1. Diffusion: holes diffuse p → n and electrons n → p (concentration gradient) → diffusion current.
  2. They leave behind immobile ions: negative acceptor ions on the p-side, positive donor ions on the n-side → a depletion region free of mobile carriers.
  3. These ions create an electric field from n to p, which drives minority carriers across → drift current.
  4. Equilibrium: diffusion current = drift current; the potential difference across the junction is the barrier potential (≈ 0.7 V Si, 0.3 V Ge).

4. Forward & reverse bias, I–V characteristics

Q. Draw circuit diagrams for forward and reverse bias and the I–V characteristic of a p–n junction diode. What is knee/threshold voltage?3–5 M

  • Forward bias (p to +, n to −): applied V opposes V₀ → barrier lowers, depletion layer narrows; current rises sharply after the knee voltage (current in mA, due to majority carriers).
  • Reverse bias: barrier increases, depletion layer widens; only a tiny reverse saturation current (μA) from minority carriers, nearly independent of voltage, until breakdown.

Dynamic resistance rd = ΔV/ΔI. E.g. ΔV = 0.1 V, ΔI = 20 mA → 5 Ω.

5. Rectifiers

Q. With a circuit diagram, explain the working of a full-wave rectifier. Draw input and output waveforms.3–5 M · most asked

Principle: a diode conducts only in forward bias.

Working: during the positive half-cycle, the top end of the secondary is +, D₁ is forward biased and conducts; D₂ is reverse biased. In the negative half-cycle, D₂ conducts and D₁ is off. In both halves the current through RL flows in the same direction → unidirectional (pulsating DC) output.

  • Half-wave: only one diode; output for one half-cycle. Output frequency = input frequency (50 Hz).
  • Full-wave: output frequency = 2 × input (100 Hz for 50 Hz mains).
  • A capacitor filter across RL smooths the ripple (charges at peaks, discharges slowly between them).
